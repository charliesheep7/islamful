Wudu means washing with water, so there is no way to do wudu itself without water. What Islam gives you instead is **tayammum**: a dry purification with clean earth that stands in for wudu, and for ghusl too, when water cannot be found or would harm you.

The Qur'an sets this out directly. After describing wudu and ghusl, it says that if you are ill, on a journey, or have relieved yourselves or been intimate and _cannot find water_, then purify yourselves with clean earth by wiping your faces and hands ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6); see also [Surah An-Nisa 4:43](https://quran.com/4/43)).

## When you can use tayammum

Tayammum is a concession for a real need, not a shortcut. The sources point to two kinds of situation:

- **No water can be found.** This is the case named in both verses, such as a journey with no water source.
- **Using water would harm you.** The verses mention illness. Amr ibn al-As had a sexual dream on a freezing night during a campaign and feared that washing would kill him, so he made tayammum and led the dawn prayer. When he explained his reason, the Prophet ﷺ laughed and did not object ([Sunan Abi Dawud 334](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:334), graded sahih by al-Albani).

If water is available and safe to use, use it. Where your situation is borderline, such as a wound or a medical instruction to keep part of the body dry, ask a scholar you trust about your specific case.

## How to perform tayammum step by step

Ammar ibn Yasir once became junub on a journey with no water and rolled himself in the dust before praying. The Prophet ﷺ told him a simpler action would have been enough, then demonstrated it: he struck the earth lightly with his hands, blew off the dust, and passed his hands over his face and hands ([Sahih al-Bukhari 338](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:338)).

1. **Make your intention** in your heart to purify yourself for prayer.
2. **Strike clean earth lightly** with both palms.
3. **Blow off the excess** so only a light trace remains.
4. **Wipe your face** with both palms.
5. **Wipe your hands**, the back of each hand with the other palm.

That is the whole act. The schools of law differ on some details, such as whether a second strike is used and whether the arms are wiped up to the elbows. The Bukhari narration describes one strike and the face and hands. If you follow a particular madhab, follow its method.

## What counts as clean earth

The verses use the phrase _sa'id tayyib_, clean or pure earth. The Prophet ﷺ listed among the things given to him that the earth was made for his followers a place to pray and a means of tayammum, so that any of them can pray wherever the time of prayer arrives ([Sahih al-Bukhari 335](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:335)).

Natural soil, sand and dust are the clearest cases. Scholars differ over surfaces such as bare stone with no dust on it. When you can, choose ground or a surface with dust on it.

## It replaces ghusl as well

Both Ammar ibn Yasir and Amr ibn al-As were in a state of major impurity (janabah) when they made tayammum, and both reports above show it was accepted in place of a full bath. When water becomes available again, perform ghusl before your next prayer that needs it.

## How long tayammum lasts

The Prophet ﷺ said that clean earth is the wudu of the Muslim even if he does not find water for ten years ([Sunan an-Nasa'i 322](https://sunnah.com/nasai:322), graded hasan). Tayammum is treated as your purification for as long as the excuse remains, so the things that break wudu, such as using the toilet, passing wind or deep sleep, end it too.

Scholars differ on whether one tayammum covers several obligatory prayers or must be renewed for each. An-Nasa'i placed the hadith above under the chapter heading _Several Prayers With One Tayammum_. Follow your school here.

**If you find water after praying.** Two companions on a journey prayed with tayammum and then found water while the prayer time was still in. One repeated his ablution and prayer and the other did not. The Prophet ﷺ told the one who did not repeat that he had followed the sunnah and his prayer was enough, and told the other that he had a double reward ([Sunan Abi Dawud 338](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:338)). Al-Albani graded it sahih, while Abu Dawud noted a disagreement over whether its chain reaches a companion.

## A worked example

You are hiking, Asr comes in, and the only water you carry is what you need to drink. You find dry, clean ground, make tayammum as above and pray Asr on time. An hour later you reach a tap. Your Asr prayer stands. For Maghrib, make wudu with water as usual.
