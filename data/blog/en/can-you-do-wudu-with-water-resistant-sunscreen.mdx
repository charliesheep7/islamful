![Pale cream title card reading Wudu With Water Resistant Sunscreen](/static/images/blog/can-you-do-wudu-with-water-resistant-sunscreen.webp)

Yes. Darul Ifta Birmingham says wudu with water resistant sunscreen is valid, as long as you have not applied it so thickly that it forms a layer stopping water from reaching your skin. Waterproof products are treated differently and need to come off first.

The test is not the word on the bottle. It is whether water actually reaches the skin of the parts you wash. This guide relies mainly on answers from one Hanafi fatwa service, so scholars elsewhere may draw the line differently.

## Quick answer

| Product                                    | Wudu with it on?                  |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Water resistant sunscreen, normal amount   | Valid                             |
| Water resistant sunscreen in a thick layer | Not until the layer is washed off |
| Waterproof product                         | Remove it first                   |
| Ordinary cream or lotion sunscreen         | Valid                             |
| Mineral sunscreen                          | Check the product; see below      |

## Why water reaching the skin matters

The verse on wudu commands washing the face, the hands to the elbows and the feet, and wiping the head ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). Even a small dry patch matters. A man who missed a fingernail-sized spot was commanded to repeat his ablution and prayer ([Sunan Ibn Majah 666](https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:666), graded sahih by Darussalam), and Abu Hurayrah urged people to perform ablution thoroughly, quoting the Prophet ﷺ: "Save your heels from the Hell-fire" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 165](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:165)).

Egypt's Dar al-Ifta states the general rule: when a substance covers part of the washed area and prevents water from reaching the skin, ablution is invalid because it remains incomplete. It also explains that something which only stains, such as henna, does not affect ablution ([fatwa 7844](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7844/why-women-are-obliged-to-remove-their-nail-polish-in-ablution-while-they-are-all)).

## Water resistant versus waterproof

Darul Ifta Birmingham's answer, written by Alimah Zainab Nakhuda and approved by Mufti Mohammed Tosir Miah, draws the key line ([fatwa 06208](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/?p=25904)):

- **Waterproof substances** completely prevent water from reaching the skin, so they need to be removed before wudu.
- **Water resistant creams** only stay on the skin against a small amount of moisture, such as sweat or light rain. When fully wet, they wash off like any other cream, so water reaches the skin.

Health advice points the same way. The NHS says water washes sunscreen off and that it should be reapplied straight after you have been in water, even if it is water resistant ([NHS sun safety](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/seasonal-health/sunscreen-and-sun-safety/)). That guidance is about sun protection, not wudu, but it shows that water resistant does not mean sealed.

The condition in the fatwa is about quantity. If you have put on a lot, take care washing, and make sure it has not become a thick layer.

## Where the answers are more cautious

The service does not give every product a blanket pass:

- **Check the ingredients.** Asked about mineral and chemical sunscreen, Alimah Zakiratul Hoque wrote that many sunscreens contain water-resistant ingredients, that mineral ones tend to be less water-resistant, and that the manufacturer is best placed to say whether a product stops water reaching the skin ([fatwa 06531](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/?p=27455)).
- **Silicone in large amounts.** Maulana Mujahid Hussain said silicone can form a barrier in large quantities, but the amount in daily products can generally be washed away. He advised applying silicone products after the obligatory parts of wudu ([fatwa 04614](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/silicone-products/)).
- **A working test.** The same answer says a barrier invalidates wudu if it is thick, visible and you can tell it stops water, as with nail polish. Vaseline and oil leave a film but do not stop water.
- **Creams in general.** Another answer, to a person who also asked about sunscreen, says oils, creams and lotions do not fall under the ruling for substances that block water ([fatwa on makeup barriers](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/how-to-differentiate-between-make-up-that-prevents-water-reaching-the-skin-and-makeup-that-doesnt/)).

These answers fit together if you treat the sunscreen on your skin as a question of fact: normally water gets through, and a thick or waterproof layer is the exception. That reading is ours. For a product you use every day, ask the manufacturer what the fatwa suggests, or ask a scholar of your school.

## How to check your sunscreen

1. Read the label. Water resistant is the case covered above. If it says waterproof, treat it as a barrier and remove it.
2. Look at how it sits. Rubbed in with nothing visible is very different from a thick white layer on the nose or cheeks.
3. Watch the water when you wash. If it runs off without wetting a patch of skin, rub that spot with your wet hand or wash the product off with a cleanser. This is a practical tip of ours, not a ruling.

## A worked example

You put on factor 50 water resistant sunscreen at the beach at 10am, rubbed in fully. Dhuhr comes in and your wudu has broken.

1. Find water and make [wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu) as normal, rubbing your face and arms as you wash so the water really covers the skin.
2. If you see a thick streak of sunscreen, wash it off that spot first.
3. Reapply your sunscreen once you have finished, since the NHS advises reapplying after contact with water and about every 2 hours.

If you have a thick layer on, or a waterproof product, clean it off before you start.

## The simplest option: wudu first

Darul Ifta Birmingham says that wudu can be done before applying makeup and that you can then pray with it on as long as your wudu has not broken ([same fatwa](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/how-to-differentiate-between-make-up-that-prevents-water-reaching-the-skin-and-makeup-that-doesnt/)). The same logic works for sunscreen. Make wudu in the morning, then apply it, and it does not become a question unless your wudu breaks. See [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu) for what does end it.

## If you find sunscreen after you have prayed

A thin trace of water resistant sunscreen falls under the fatwa above, so there is nothing to redo. If a waterproof product or a thick layer clearly stopped water from reaching part of your face or arms, the cautious course is to repeat your wudu and that prayer, given the report in Ibn Majah. If you keep doubting yourself over this, ask a scholar how far you need to go.

## Your next step

On hot days and beach trips, planning helps. Check today's [prayer times](/prayer-times) so you can make wudu before you put your sunscreen on, or know when you will need to reapply it after the next prayer.
