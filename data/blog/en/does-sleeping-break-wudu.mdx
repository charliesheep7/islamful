**Sleep can break wudu, but feeling drowsy is not the same as losing awareness in sleep.** How you were sitting or lying down also matters in the school-specific guidance below. After ordinary sleep lying in bed, renew wudu before prayer; do not assume the same answer applies to every moment of nodding off.

For the washing sequence itself, use our [step-by-step wudu guide](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).

## Drowsiness is different from deeper sleep

In [Sunan Abi Dawud 200](https://sunnah.com/abudawud/1/200), Anas describes the Companions of Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) dozing as they waited for Isha, then praying without repeating ablution. The linked edition records al-Albani’s grading as sahih. The report is one reason not to treat every brief nod of the head as an automatic loss of wudu.

[Dar al-Ifta’s answer about drowsiness in prayer](https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/17475/%D8%AA%D8%A3%D8%AB%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%B3-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%89-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A9) distinguishes someone still aware of surrounding speech from someone whose sleep has overcome that awareness. Its discussion concerns the nature of the sleep, not a stopwatch measurement.

## Why the school-specific details matter

The **Hanafi explanation from [Darul Ifta Birmingham](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/does-light-sleeping-break-the-wudhu/)** distinguishes a firmly seated position from lying down or leaning so heavily on support that removing it would make the sleeper fall. Its answer says wudu remains with the seated condition it describes, while the lying or dependent-leaning cases invalidate it. Sitting somewhere does not, by itself, establish that all of those conditions were met.

The **Maliki passage quoted by Dar al-Ifta** emphasizes heavy versus light sleep: heavy sleep invalidates wudu even when brief, whereas light sleep is treated differently even when longer. That is why “I only slept for a few minutes” is not enough information to apply this position.

These are specific distinctions from the cited sources, not a complete chart of every school’s exceptions. If you follow a particular school, describe both your awareness and your posture when asking a teacher about an unclear case.

## Apply the distinction to your situation

| What happened                                                        | What to consider                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| You felt sleepy while listening, but remained aware of what was said | This resembles the drowsiness discussed by Dar al-Ifta; mere tiredness is not the same as deep sleep.                         |
| You nodded off while seated on a chair                               | Under the cited Hanafi answer, check the seated condition and dependence on support rather than using the word “chair” alone. |
| You slept lying on your bed or side                                  | The cited Hanafi guidance treats this as breaking wudu. Renew it before prayer.                                               |

For example, two people might both call their experience a “short nap”: one remained aware while sitting upright; the other lay down and slept. Those descriptions give a teacher useful information. Saying only that both naps lasted ten minutes leaves out the details used in these rulings.

Avoid turning this into repeated self-testing. Dar al-Ifta quotes the Hanbali scholar Ibn Qudamah on uncertainty about whether sleep occurred: that doubt alone does not require wudu. This is a statement about uncertainty, not permission to disregard sleep you know occurred.

If another event happened too, consider its own ruling. A bleeding cut, for example, raises a separate question covered in [does bleeding break wudu?](/blog/does-bleeding-break-wudu).

When you are ready for salah, check [prayer times for your location](/prayer-times). Leave enough time for ablution when planning a rest before prayer.
