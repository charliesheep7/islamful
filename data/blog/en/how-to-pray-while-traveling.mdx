Airports, motorways and train stations were not built around prayer times. Islam knew that long before any of them existed, which is why travel comes with two built-in concessions: shortening your prayers and combining them.

> **Quick answer:** When you travel roughly 80 km or more from home, you pray Dhuhr, Asr and Isha as **two rakahs** instead of four (called _qasr_), while Fajr and Maghrib stay the same. Most schools also let you **combine** Dhuhr with Asr and Maghrib with Isha. The Prophet ﷺ called shortening "a charity which Allah has given you, so accept His charity" (Narrated by Muslim, 686).

**What you'll learn:**

- Who counts as a traveler, and for how long
- How to shorten and combine each prayer, step by step
- How the four madhabs differ, and how to pray on a plane or train

## Why does Islam make prayer easier on a journey?

Travel prayer is not a loophole. It comes directly from the Quran. Allah (SWT) says in Surah An-Nisa, 4:101:

> وَإِذَا ضَرَبْتُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ فَلَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ أَن تَقْصُرُوا مِنَ الصَّلَاةِ
>
> _Wa idha darabtum fil-ardi fa laysa 'alaykum junahun an taqsuru minas-salah_
>
> "And when you travel throughout the land, there is no blame upon you for shortening the prayer."

The verse goes on to mention fear of attack, which puzzled the Companions once travel became safe. Ya'la ibn Umayyah raised this with Umar ibn al-Khattab, who had asked the Prophet ﷺ the same question. The answer was that shortening is "a charity which Allah has given you, so accept His charity" (Narrated by Muslim, 686). You can read the full verse on [Quran.com, 4:101](https://quran.com/4/101).

Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) added that prayer was originally prescribed as two rakahs; the travel prayer stayed that way, and the prayer at home was increased (Narrated by Bukhari, 1090).

So the question is not whether you are "allowed" to pray less. It is a gift, and the Prophet ﷺ told us to accept it.

## Before you begin: are you a traveler?

The concessions apply once you meet two conditions: distance and duration.

### How far do you need to go?

The majority of scholars put the minimum at about **80 km (50 miles) one way**. [IslamQA explains the distance](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/105844) and notes that it is measured on the outward journey, not the round trip. Some scholars, including Ibn Taymiyyah, held that the deciding factor is whether people would customarily call it a journey.

You begin shortening **once you have left the built-up area of your town**, not while you are packing at home. You stop when you arrive back.

### How long can you keep shortening?

It depends on how long you **intend** to stay at your destination:

- **Four days or fewer** (Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali): keep shortening
- **More than four days**: pray in full from arrival
- **Unsure when you will leave** (waiting for a visa, a repair, a meeting that keeps moving): many scholars say you keep shortening for as long as that uncertainty lasts (the Shafi'i school caps it at eighteen days)
- **Hanafi school**: the cut-off is an intended stay of fifteen days

If you are going somewhere you consider home, such as your parents' town where you also live part of the year, scholars generally say you pray in full there.

## How to shorten your prayers (qasr), step by step

Shortening only affects the three four-rakah prayers. Everything else about the prayer stays exactly the same.

| Prayer  | At home   | While traveling |
| ------- | --------- | --------------- |
| Fajr    | 2 rakahs  | 2 rakahs        |
| Dhuhr   | 4 rakahs  | 2 rakahs        |
| Asr     | 4 rakahs  | 2 rakahs        |
| Maghrib | 3 rakahs  | 3 rakahs        |
| Isha    | 4 rakahs  | 2 rakahs        |

1. **Make wudu.** If water is unavailable on your journey, [tayammum](/blog/how-to-perform-tayammum) takes its place.
2. **Find the qiblah** and check the prayer time where you actually are, not back home. Our [prayer times tool](/prayer-times) updates for your current location.
3. **Make the intention** in your heart to pray, for example, Dhuhr as a traveler's two rakahs.
4. **Pray two rakahs** exactly as you would pray Fajr: takbir, Al-Fatihah and a surah, ruku, sujud, sit for the tashahhud and salawat, then end with the taslim.
5. **Do not add a third rakah.** The prayer is complete after two.

**Common mistake:** shortening Maghrib to two. Maghrib always stays three rakahs, at home or away.

### What if you pray behind a local imam?

If you join a congregation led by a resident who prays four rakahs, the majority of scholars say you **pray all four with him**. When Ibn Abbas was asked how to pray in Makkah when not praying behind the imam, he said, "Two rakahs, the Sunnah of Abul-Qasim ﷺ" (Narrated by Muslim, 688). The implication, which scholars drew out, is that behind a resident imam the traveler follows the imam.

## How to combine prayers (jam'), step by step

Combining means praying two prayers back to back in the time of one of them. There are two pairs, and they never cross over.

- **Dhuhr + Asr** — in Dhuhr time (early) or Asr time (late)
- **Maghrib + Isha** — in Maghrib time (early) or Isha time (late)

Fajr is never combined with anything. Ibn Abbas reported that the Prophet ﷺ used to combine Dhuhr and Asr on journeys, and Maghrib and Isha as well (Narrated by Bukhari, 1107).

### Combining early (jam' taqdim)

1. When Dhuhr time comes in, pray Dhuhr as two rakahs.
2. Straight away, stand and pray Asr as two rakahs.
3. You are done until Maghrib time. Do not pray Asr again later.

### Combining late (jam' ta'khir)

1. Let Dhuhr time pass while you travel, intending to combine it with Asr.
2. When Asr time comes in, pray Dhuhr first as two rakahs.
3. Then pray Asr as two rakahs.

The same steps apply to Maghrib and Isha, except Maghrib stays three rakahs. Keep the order: the earlier prayer always comes first. For a refresher on the individual prayers, see [how to pray Dhuhr](/blog/how-to-pray-dhuhr) and [how to pray Isha](/blog/how-to-pray-isha).

You can shorten without combining, and many scholars say that is better when you have stopped somewhere and are not under pressure. Combining is most useful while you are actually on the move.

## How do the four madhabs differ?

The broad picture is the same across the schools, but the details matter if you follow one of them.

| Aspect                    | Hanafi                                  | Maliki                  | Shafi'i                                 | Hanbali                        |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Ruling on shortening      | Required for the traveler               | Confirmed sunnah        | Permitted, and preferred on long trips  | Preferred over praying in full |
| Minimum distance          | About three days of travel (around 80 km) | About 80 km           | About 80 km                             | About 80 km                    |
| Stay that ends the concession | Intending 15 days or more           | Intending more than 4 days | Intending more than 4 days           | Intending more than 4 days     |
| Combining on a journey    | Only at Arafah and Muzdalifah           | Allowed                 | Allowed                                 | Allowed                        |

Hanafis who cannot combine often pray Dhuhr at the very end of its time and Asr at the very start of its time. It looks like combining, but each prayer is still inside its own window.

## Can you pray on a plane, train or in a car?

For **voluntary prayers**, yes, in whatever direction you are facing. Jabir ibn Abdullah reported that the Prophet ﷺ prayed voluntary prayers on his mount facing east, but for the obligatory prayer he would dismount and face the qiblah (Narrated by Bukhari, 1099).

For **obligatory prayers**, try these options in order:

1. **Combine** so you can pray both prayers on the ground before departure or after landing.
2. If the prayer time will pass before you land, **pray on board**. Stand facing the qiblah if there is space, such as near the galley.
3. If you cannot stand or turn, **pray seated**, lowering your head for ruku and a little further for sujud.

A driver should pull over rather than pray behind the wheel. Travel is a reason for ease, not for praying unsafely.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Shortening before you leave town.** The concession starts once you pass the edge of your city, not the night before.
- **Shortening Maghrib or Fajr.** Only the four-rakah prayers become two.
- **Mixing up the pairs.** Asr and Maghrib cannot be combined, and neither can Isha and Fajr.
- **Skipping prayers "because I'm traveling".** Shortening and combining exist precisely so you never have to miss one. If you already have, our guide on [how to make up missed prayers](/blog/how-to-make-up-missed-prayers) explains what to do.
- **Keeping the concession after settling.** Once you intend to stay beyond the limit your school sets, go back to full prayers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Do you pray sunnah prayers while traveling?**
The Prophet ﷺ was known to keep the two sunnah rakahs before Fajr and the Witr prayer on journeys, while the regular sunnahs attached to Dhuhr, Maghrib and Isha are generally dropped. You can still pray any voluntary prayer you like.

**Q: Is it better to shorten or pray in full?**
Most scholars say shortening is better, because the Prophet ﷺ described it as a charity from Allah to be accepted (Narrated by Muslim, 686). In the Hanafi school, shortening is required.

**Q: Can you combine prayers without traveling?**
Ibn Abbas reported that the Prophet ﷺ once combined prayers in Madinah without fear or travel, explaining that "he intended that no one from his community would be burdened" (Narrated by Muslim, 705). Many scholars allow it for genuine hardship, such as illness, but not as a habit.

## Summary

- Travel roughly 80 km or more, and shortening begins once you leave your town
- Pray Dhuhr, Asr and Isha as two rakahs; Fajr and Maghrib stay the same
- Combine Dhuhr with Asr, or Maghrib with Isha, early or late (except in the Hanafi school)
- Pray in full once you intend to stay more than four days, or fifteen for Hanafis
- On a plane, combine where you can, and pray seated if you must

Before you set off, it is also sunnah to recite the [dua for travel](/blog/dua-for-travel). Safe journey, and may Allah accept your prayers wherever you are.
