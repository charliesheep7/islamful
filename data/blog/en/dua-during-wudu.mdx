The authentic remembrance for wudu comes at two points: say **Bismillah** as you begin, and say the **shahada** when you finish. The narrations cited here do not give a separate dua for each limb you wash. Below you will find the Arabic, the meaning, where each wording comes from and how strong its chain is.

## The short answer

| When                    | What to say                                                                 | Source                                    |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Before you start        | Bismillah                                                                   | Abu Dawud 101, Tirmidhi 25, Ibn Majah 397 |
| After you finish        | Ashhadu an la ilaha illallah, wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan 'abduhu wa rasuluh | Nasa'i 148 (sahih)                        |
| Optional addition after | Allahumma-j'alni minat-tawwabin, waj'alni minal-mutatahhirin                | Tirmidhi 55 (weak chain)                  |
| While washing each limb | No specific wording in these narrations                                     | See below                                 |

## Before wudu: Bismillah

**بِسْمِ اللَّهِ**

_Bismillah_ means "in the name of Allah."

The Prophet ﷺ said: "There is no wudu for one who does not mention Allah's name over it" ([Jami' at-Tirmidhi 25](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:25)). The same wording comes through Abu Sa'id in [Sunan Ibn Majah 397](https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:397), graded hasan, and through Abu Hurairah in [Sunan Abi Dawud 101](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:101), graded sahih by al-Albani.

Scholars read these reports differently. In his notes on hadith 25, Imam at-Tirmidhi quotes Ahmad ibn Hanbal saying he knew of no hadith on this topic with a good chain. He quotes Ishaq ibn Rahwayh saying that someone who leaves the tasmiyah deliberately should repeat wudu, while someone who forgets it has a valid wudu. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta includes saying Bismillah, together with the intention in your heart, as the first step of wudu ([Dar al-Ifta, How to perform Wudhu](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/249/how-to-perform-wudhu-ablution)).

In practice: say Bismillah every time. If you forget, follow the view of your school or ask a scholar you trust before repeating a completed wudu.

## After wudu: the shahada

**أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لاَ إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ**

_Ashhadu an la ilaha illallah, wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan 'abduhu wa rasuluh._

"I bear witness that there is none worthy of worship except Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His slave and Messenger."

Umar ibn al-Khattab reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said whoever does wudu, does it well, and then says these words will have the eight gates of Paradise opened for him, to enter by whichever he wishes ([Sunan an-Nasa'i 148](https://sunnah.com/nasai:148), graded sahih). Dar al-Ifta gives the same words as the closing step of wudu.

### The longer version

[Jami' at-Tirmidhi 55](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:55) reports the same hadith with _wahdahu la sharika lah_ (alone, with no partner) and an extra line:

**اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنِي مِنَ التَّوَّابِينَ وَاجْعَلْنِي مِنَ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ**

_Allahumma-j'alni minat-tawwabin, waj'alni minal-mutatahhirin._

"O Allah, make me among those who repent and make me among those who purify themselves."

Imam at-Tirmidhi notes that this chain is inconsistent (mudtarib), and Sunnah.com shows it graded da'if. The core shahada is established by Nasa'i 148. Many Muslims add the extra line as a general dua; if you do, know that its chain is weaker than the shahada itself.

## Is there a dua for each limb?

Many wudu booklets and posters give a different dua for the face, the arms, the head and the feet. Those wordings are not found in any of the narrations above. Dar al-Ifta's step-by-step guide gives Bismillah at the start, the shahada at the end, and nothing in between. Scholars have disagreed about whether the limb-by-limb duas have a reliable basis, so do not present them as the Prophet's ﷺ wording or worry that your wudu is incomplete without them.

The Quran describes the purpose of wudu itself: "It is not Allah's Will to burden you, but to purify you and complete His favour upon you, so perhaps you will be grateful" ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). Washing carefully and with presence is the act of worship.

### "Allahummaghfir li dhanbi"

A popular dua said during wudu is _Allahummaghfir li dhanbi, wa wassi' li fi dari, wa barik li fima razaqtani_ ("O Allah, forgive my sin, make my home spacious for me, and bless me in what You have provided me"). In [Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3500](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3500) a man reports hearing it from the Prophet ﷺ at night; that report does not mention wudu, and it is graded da'if. It is a beautiful request for forgiveness, but it is not established as a dua specific to wudu.

## A simple routine

1. Make the intention in your heart and say **Bismillah**.
2. Wash each part in order. For the full steps, see [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).
3. When you finish, say the **shahada**.
4. Pray, or keep your wudu for the next salah. Check the time with Islamful's [prayer times](/prayer-times).
