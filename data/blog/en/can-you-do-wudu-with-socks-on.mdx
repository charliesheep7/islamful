![Cream title card reading Can You Do Wudu With Socks On?](/static/images/blog/can-you-do-wudu-with-socks-on.webp)

Yes, in some cases you can complete wudu without taking your socks off. Instead of washing your feet, you wipe a wet hand over the top of the socks. This is called **mash** (wiping), and the Prophet ﷺ did it over his khuffs, leather socks, on his journeys.

It only works if certain conditions are met, and scholars differ on whether ordinary cloth socks count. If the conditions are not met, take your socks off and wash your feet as the Qur'an commands: wash your faces, hands to the elbows, wipe your heads, and wash your feet to the ankles ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)).

## The evidence for wiping over khuffs

Al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah was travelling with the Prophet ﷺ and poured water for him while he made wudu. He washed his face and forearms, wiped his head, and wiped over his two khuffs ([Sahih al-Bukhari 182](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:182)).

On another journey, al-Mughirah reached down to take the Prophet's khuffs off. The Prophet ﷺ told him to leave them, because he had put them on while his feet were pure, and then wiped over them ([Sahih al-Bukhari 206](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:206)).

That second report gives the key condition: **the socks must have been put on while you were already in a state of wudu.**

## Leather khuffs versus ordinary socks

Wiping over leather khuffs is established in the authentic reports above. Ordinary fabric socks (_jawrab_) are where the disagreement lies.

Al-Mughirah also reported that the Prophet ﷺ made wudu and wiped over his socks and sandals ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 99](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:99)). At-Tirmidhi graded it hasan sahih, but Darussalam's grading shown on the same page is da'if, so its strength is disputed.

At-Tirmidhi then records the views of jurists. Sufyan ath-Thawri, Ibn al-Mubarak, ash-Shafi'i, Ahmad and Ishaq said one may wipe over socks even without sandals, **as long as they are thick**. He also relates that Abu Hanifa, in his final illness, wiped over socks without leather soles and said he had done something that day he used not to do.

What this means in practice:

- **Leather khuffs, or thick socks that cover the ankle:** wiping is widely accepted.
- **Thin cotton or nylon socks:** many scholars do not accept wiping over these. Some contemporary scholars are more lenient. Follow your madhab or a scholar you trust here.
- **Unsure?** Taking off your socks and washing your feet is valid in every view.

## How long the wiping lasts

Safwan ibn Assal said that when they were travelling, the Prophet ﷺ told them not to remove their khuffs for three days and nights, whether after using the toilet or sleeping, except in a state of janabah ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 96](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:96); also [Sunan an-Nasa'i 127](https://sunnah.com/nasai:127), both graded hasan).

At-Tirmidhi adds that most companions and later jurists, including ath-Thawri, Ibn al-Mubarak, ash-Shafi'i, Ahmad and Ishaq, held:

- **Resident:** one day and night.
- **Traveller:** three days and nights.

He also notes that some scholars set no time limit. Jurists differ on exactly when the period begins, so check your school's position if you rely on the full period.

## When wiping no longer applies

- **Janabah.** Safwan's report excludes it. After sexual impurity you must take the socks off and do a full [ghusl](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl).
- **The time period ends.** Take the socks off and wash your feet in your next wudu.
- **The socks were put on without wudu.** Wiping was never valid for that pair. Wash your feet.

Ordinary things that break wudu, such as using the toilet, sleep or [passing wind](/blog/does-passing-wind-break-wudu), only break your wudu. You can still wipe over the socks in the next one while the period lasts, as Safwan's report shows.

## How to wipe over your socks

1. Do the steps of [wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu) as usual up to and including wiping your head.
2. Wet your hands.
3. Wipe once over the **top** of each sock, from the toes towards the ankle. Ali ibn Abi Talib said that if religion were based on opinion, the underside would be more fitting to wipe, but he saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ wipe the upper part ([Sunan Abi Dawud 162](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:162), graded sahih by al-Albani).
4. Do not wipe the sole or the heel, and do not wash the socks.

## A worked example

You make full wudu at home before Fajr and then put on a pair of thick socks. At work, before Dhuhr, you use the toilet and need wudu again. Do the steps as normal, then wipe over the tops of your socks instead of taking them off. If you are not travelling, the majority view gives you one day and night of wiping. Once that ends, take the socks off and wash your feet.

## Your next step

Wiping over socks is most useful at work, at school and on the road, when finding a place to wash your feet before a prayer is hard. Check today's [prayer times](/prayer-times) so you know which prayers fall during your day, and plan your wudu around them. If you are travelling and water is scarce, see [how to perform tayammum](/blog/how-to-perform-tayammum).
