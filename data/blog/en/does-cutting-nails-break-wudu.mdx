![Green illustration with the title Does Cutting Nails Break Wudu](/static/images/blog/does-cutting-nails-break-wudu.webp)

No, cutting your nails does not break wudu. You can trim your fingernails or toenails and go straight to prayer with the wudu you already have. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta adds one courtesy: it is preferable to wash your hands afterwards.

Two situations are worth knowing about: cutting so close that you bleed, and wearing nail polish when you next make wudu.

## What the scholars say

Asked whether clipping nails nullifies ablution, Egypt's Dar al-Ifta answered: "No, it does not, and it is preferable to wash the hands afterwards" ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 7859](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7859/does-the-clipping-of-nails-nullify-ablution)). It gave the same answer in a separate fatwa ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 8093](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/8093/clipping-nails-and-ablution)).

Darul Ifta Birmingham, a Hanafi institute, is just as direct: cutting nails or removing unwanted hair does not break wudu ([Darul Ifta Birmingham](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/does-cutting-nails-or-hair-break-wudhu/)).

The reason is simple. Wudu is broken by specific things, and trimming nails is not one of them. In a related answer, Darul Ifta Birmingham lists the Hanafi nullifiers: anything that leaves the front or back passage, blood or pus that flows from its place, a mouthful of vomit, sleeping while lying or leaning, losing consciousness or sanity, and laughing aloud in a prayer with ruku and sujud ([Darul Ifta Birmingham on waxing and tweezing](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/does-a-woman-need-to-repeat-her-wudu-if-she-waxes-her-body-or-tweezes-her-hair-from-the-root/)). Other schools have their own lists; see [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu) for an overview.

## Exception 1: if you cut too deep and bleed

Trimming is not the problem, but blood can be. In the same fatwa, Darul Ifta Birmingham says that waxing or tweezing does not break wudu, but if blood comes out and moves from its place afterwards, wudu is broken. The same Hanafi rule applies if you nick the skin beside a nail and the blood flows.

Schools differ on whether bleeding breaks wudu at all, so read [does bleeding break wudu](/blog/does-bleeding-break-wudu) and follow the view of your school.

## Exception 2: nail polish at your next wudu

Painting or wearing nail polish does not break wudu. The question is what happens when you next need to make wudu. Dar al-Ifta says that if the polish prevents water from reaching the nail, it is obligatory to remove it before making wudu. If it does not block water, like dye and henna, it does not need removing ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 6125](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6125/can-i-pray-with-my-nail-polish-or-should-i-remove-it-for-ablution)).

The Quran commands washing the hands up to the elbows and the feet to the ankles ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)), which is why water has to reach the nails themselves.

## Nail care is part of the Sunnah

Cutting nails is one of the acts of the fitrah. Anas reported that a limit was set for trimming the moustache, cutting the nails, removing armpit hair and shaving pubic hair: they should not be left for more than forty nights ([Sahih Muslim 258](https://sunnah.com/muslim:258)).

## A simple routine

1. Trim your nails whenever is convenient, with or without wudu.
2. Wash your hands afterwards, as Dar al-Ifta recommends.
3. If you drew blood, check whether it flowed and follow your school's ruling on bleeding.
4. Remove water-blocking polish before your next wudu. For the full steps, see [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).
5. Plan nail care for a gap between prayers, using Islamful's [prayer times](/prayer-times) to see how long you have before the next one.
