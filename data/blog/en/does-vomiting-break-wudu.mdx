![Green illustration with the title Does Vomiting Break Wudu](/static/images/blog/does-vomiting-break-wudu.webp)

Whether vomiting breaks wudu depends on the school of Islamic law you follow. In the Hanafi school, vomiting a mouthful breaks wudu. Imam Malik and Imam ash-Shafi'i held that vomiting does not break wudu at all. On every view, vomit is something you clean from your mouth, body and clothes before you pray.

## The short answer

- **Hanafi:** a mouthful of vomit breaks wudu. A smaller amount does not.
- **Maliki and Shafi'i:** vomiting does not break wudu.
- **Ahmad and others:** Imam at-Tirmidhi lists Ahmad among the scholars who held that wudu is made after vomiting.
- **Everyone:** rinse your mouth and wash off any vomit before prayer.

## Where the difference comes from

Allah commands wudu when you rise to pray and mentions relieving yourself as a reason to purify again ([Surah Al-Ma'idah, 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). Vomit is not named in the verse, so the question is settled by the Sunnah and by how scholars read it.

The key narration comes from Abu ad-Darda: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ vomited and then performed wudu, and Thawban confirmed that he himself had poured the water for it ([Jami' at-Tirmidhi 87](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:87), graded hasan). Imam at-Tirmidhi then records the disagreement. A number of Companions and later scholars held that wudu is made after vomiting and nosebleeds, which was the view of Sufyan ath-Thawri, Ibn al-Mubarak, Ahmad and Ishaq. Malik and ash-Shafi'i said vomiting and nosebleeds do not require wudu.

Both positions are long-standing scholarly views. Follow the school you were taught, or the scholar you rely on, rather than picking whichever answer is easier on a given day.

## The Hanafi ruling: what counts as a mouthful

Darul Ifta Birmingham, a Hanafi institute, lists "vomiting in mouthful" among the eight things that nullify wudu ([Darul Ifta Birmingham](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/would-i-have-to-repeat-my-wudhu-if-i-broke-my-nafl-salah-to-answer-a-mobile-call/)). Because the condition is a mouthful, a smaller amount does not break wudu in this school.

In a separate answer, the same institute defines a mouthful as vomit that the mouth can only keep closed on with difficulty, or that prevents speech. The measure is the mouth of the person who vomited, so a child is judged by the child's own mouth. A mouthful of vomit is also treated as impure (najis) ([Darul Ifta Birmingham on a mouthful of vomit](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/dealing-with-waswasah-regarding-najasah-and-non-halal-meat-and-the-ruling-on-a-mouthful-of-vomit/)).

## The Maliki and Shafi'i view

For those who follow Malik or ash-Shafi'i, wudu stays valid after vomiting. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta gives the same answer: vomiting does not nullify ablution, but vomit is impurity, so you rinse your mouth and wash whatever it soiled, because prayer is only valid once impurity is removed ([Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyyah, fatwa 7861](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7861/vomiting-and-ablution)).

## What to do if you vomit before prayer

1. **Clean up first.** Rinse your mouth well and wash any vomit off your face, hands and clothes.
2. **Apply your school's ruling.** If you are Hanafi and brought up a mouthful, make wudu again. If you follow the Maliki or Shafi'i view, your wudu still stands.
3. **When unsure, renew it.** Darul Ifta Birmingham names wudu after vomiting as one of the matters where an imam should take care, because it is obligatory in some schools and not in others ([Darul Ifta Birmingham on following an imam of another school](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/ruling-on-making-iqtida-of-an-imam-when-his-actions-differ-from-ones-own-madhhab/)). Renewing your wudu is valid on every view.
4. **Watch the time.** Check today's [prayer times](/prayer-times) so you can rest, clean up and still pray before the time ends.

If you need a refresher on the steps, see [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).

## Don't let doubt take over

Illness can bring worry about whether everything is clean. Darul Ifta Birmingham reminds people that certainty is not lifted by doubt: if you are not sure impurity reached your clothes or prayer mat, you may treat them as clean ([Darul Ifta Birmingham on a mouthful of vomit](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/dealing-with-waswasah-regarding-najasah-and-non-halal-meat-and-the-ruling-on-a-mouthful-of-vomit/)). Wash what you can see, then pray.
