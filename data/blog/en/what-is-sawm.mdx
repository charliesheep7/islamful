Every year, well over a billion people stop eating and drinking at dawn and wait until sunset. For a month. On purpose. That practice has a name in Arabic, and it is one of the pillars the whole religion stands on.

**Sawm (صَوْم) is fasting in Islam: holding back from food, drink, and marital relations from true dawn until sunset, with the intention of worshipping Allah.** Fasting the month of Ramadan is the fourth pillar of Islam. The Prophet ﷺ listed it among the five things "Islam is built upon" (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 8). It is obligatory on every adult, sane Muslim who is able to do it.

**Key Points:**

| # | Point | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meaning | Linguistically "to abstain"; in worship, no food, drink, or intimacy from dawn to sunset |
| 2 | Ruling | Ramadan fasting is fard (obligatory), the fourth pillar of Islam |

| 4 | Exemptions | The sick, travellers, menstruating women, and those permanently unable |
| 5 | Reward | Past sins forgiven for fasting Ramadan with faith and hope of reward |

Here is what sawm means, what it requires of you, and why Muslims take it so seriously.

## What Does Sawm Mean?

The Arabic root **ص-و-م** (s-w-m) means to hold back or abstain. Before it became a technical term, Arabs used it for any kind of restraint, even a horse standing still.

The Quran shows this wider meaning in the story of Maryam. After giving birth to Isa (peace be upon him), she is told what to say to anyone who questions her:

إني نذرت للرحمن صوما فلن أكلم اليوم إنسيا

*Inni nadhartu lir-Rahmani sawman falan ukallima al-yawma insiyya*

"Indeed, I have vowed to the Most Merciful abstention, so I will not speak today to any man." ([Surah Maryam, 19:26](https://quran.com/19/26))

Her *sawm* was a fast from speech. In Islamic law, the word narrows to one specific act of worship: **abstaining from the things that break the fast, from the appearance of true dawn until sunset, with a prior intention.**

You will also see the word **siyam** (صِيَام). It comes from the same root and means the same thing. The Quran uses *siyam* in the verse that makes fasting obligatory, and Muslims use both words interchangeably.

## Why Do Muslims Fast?

The answer is in the verse that commands it:

يا أيها الذين آمنوا كتب عليكم الصيام كما كتب على الذين من قبلكم لعلكم تتقون

*Ya ayyuha alladhina amanu kutiba 'alaykumu as-siyamu kama kutiba 'ala alladhina min qablikum la'allakum tattaqun*

"O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you, that you may become righteous." ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:183](https://quran.com/2/183))

Two things stand out in that verse.

- **Fasting is not new.** Allah ﷻ tells you it was prescribed for earlier communities too. Muslims are joining a long line of people who fasted for God.
- **The goal is taqwa.** *La'allakum tattaqun* means "so that you may become conscious of Allah" or "righteous." Hunger is not the point. Awareness is.

That is why sawm is more than skipping meals. If you can say no to water on a hot afternoon, when no one is watching and it is lawful at any other time, you are training yourself to say no to things that are never lawful.

The Prophet ﷺ made the same point from the other side. He said that whoever does not give up false speech and acting on it, **Allah has no need of him giving up his food and drink** (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1903). A fast that leaves the tongue untouched has missed its purpose.

## Who Has to Fast, and Who Is Exempt?

Ramadan fasting is obligatory on every Muslim who is **adult, sane, resident (not travelling), and physically able**. Children are not required to fast, though many families train them gradually.

The Quran builds the exemptions into the command itself. Allah says whoever is ill or on a journey should fast "an equal number of other days," and adds: **"Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship"** ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:185](https://quran.com/2/185)).

| Situation | Fast in Ramadan? | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary illness | Not required | Make up (qada) each missed day later |
| Travelling | Optional | Make up any missed days later |
| Menstruation or postnatal bleeding | Not allowed | Make up the days later, not the prayers |
| Pregnancy or breastfeeding (if fasting risks harm) | Not required | Make up later; some scholars add fidyah |
| Old age or permanent illness | Not required | Fidyah: feed one poor person per day |

The rule for menstruating women comes from 'A'isha (may Allah be pleased with her). When asked why women make up missed fasts but not missed prayers, she explained that this was simply what they were commanded to do in the time of the Prophet ﷺ (Narrated by Muslim, 335).

The fidyah for those who cannot fast at all comes from [Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:184](https://quran.com/2/184): "a ransom of feeding a poor person" for each day.

## How Does a Day of Sawm Work?

A fast has a clear start, a clear end, and a short list of things that break it.

### The Start: Intention and Suhoor

Every fast needs an **intention (niyyah)**. This is a decision in the heart, not a sentence you have to say out loud. Waking up to eat before dawn already shows it.

That pre-dawn meal is **suhoor**, and it is a sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Take suhoor, for there is blessing in it" (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1923).

The fast begins at true dawn. The Quran describes it precisely: "eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread," then "complete the fast until the night" ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:187](https://quran.com/2/187)). In practice, you stop at the time of the fajr prayer and break at maghrib.

### The End: Iftar at Sunset

The fast ends at sunset, and you are encouraged not to delay it. The Prophet ﷺ said people will remain upon good **as long as they hasten to break the fast** (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1957). There is a sunnah [dua for breaking the fast](/blog/dua-for-breaking-fast) too.

### What Breaks the Fast?

The main things that invalidate sawm when done **deliberately**:

- **Eating or drinking** anything, including through the nose
- **Sexual intercourse**, which also requires a heavy expiation (kaffarah) in Ramadan, as in the hadith of the man who came to the Prophet ﷺ saying he was ruined (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1936)
- **Deliberately making yourself vomit**
- **The start of menstruation or postnatal bleeding**, even minutes before sunset

The key word is *deliberately*. If you eat or drink out of genuine forgetfulness, your fast is still valid. The Prophet ﷺ said to complete the fast, "for it is Allah who fed him and gave him drink" (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1933).

### When Must You Make the Intention?

The four madhabs agree the obligatory fast needs an intention, but they differ on the timing:

| Madhab | Intention for a Ramadan fast |
|---|---|
| Hanafi | Valid from the night before until before the midpoint of the day |
| Maliki | One intention at the start of Ramadan covers the whole month |
| Shafi'i | A fresh intention each night, before fajr |
| Hanbali | A fresh intention each night, before fajr |

The safest practice, which satisfies every opinion, is simply to intend each night that you will fast tomorrow.

## What Are the Types of Sawm?

Ramadan is the fast everyone knows, but Islamic law recognises several categories.

- **Obligatory (fard):** the month of Ramadan, plus making up any Ramadan days you missed (qada). Fasts owed as an expiation (kaffarah) and fasts you vowed (nadhr) are also binding.
- **Recommended (sunnah):** fasts the Prophet ﷺ encouraged. These include the day of 'Arafah, the day of 'Ashura, and Mondays and Thursdays.
- **Forbidden (haram):** fasting on the two days of Eid. The Prophet ﷺ forbade fasting on Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1991).
- **Disliked (makruh):** singling out Friday alone for a voluntary fast. The Prophet ﷺ said not to fast on Friday unless you also fast the day before or after (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1985).

A few of the recommended fasts come with specific rewards, all from one hadith of Abu Qatada in Sahih Muslim. **Fasting the day of 'Arafah** expiates the sins of the year before and the year after. **Fasting 'Ashura** expiates the year before. Asked about **Monday**, the Prophet ﷺ said it was the day he was born and the day revelation came to him (Narrated by Muslim, 1162).

Then there are the **six days of Shawwal**. Whoever fasts Ramadan and follows it with six days of Shawwal, the Prophet ﷺ said, is as though he fasted perpetually (Narrated by Muslim, 1164).

## What Rewards Are Promised for Fasting?

Sawm has a reward that is different from other acts of worship. In a hadith qudsi, Allah says:

"All the deeds of the son of Adam are for him, except fasting. It is for Me, and I will reward it." (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1904)

The same hadith calls fasting **a shield**. It says the fasting person has two joys: when they break the fast, and when they meet their Lord.

Other authentic narrations add:

- **Forgiveness.** Whoever fasts Ramadan out of faith and hoping for reward has their past sins forgiven (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 38).
- **A gate of their own.** Paradise has a gate called **Ar-Rayyan**, and only those who fasted will enter through it (Narrated by al-Bukhari, 1896).

Notice the condition in the forgiveness hadith: *imanan wa ihtisaban*, with faith and seeking reward. The promise is tied to why you fast, not just the fact that you went hungry.

## How to Make Sawm Part of Your Life

You do not have to wait for Ramadan to benefit from fasting.

- **Start small.** If you have never fasted voluntarily, try one Monday. Eat suhoor, make the intention, and notice how the day feels.
- **Protect your tongue.** Treat the fast as a day off from gossip, arguing, and complaining, not just from lunch.
- **Clear your debts.** If you owe days from a past Ramadan, make them up before the next one arrives.
- **Use the evenings.** Iftar is a good time for dua. Our [dua collection](/dua) has the supplications for fasting, iftar, and Laylat al-Qadr in Arabic with translation.
- **Pair it with Quran.** Ramadan is the month the Quran was revealed ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:185](https://quran.com/2/185)). Reading even a few verses a day adds to the fast, and our free [Quran Answers](/answers) tool shows you what the Quran says on any question you bring to it.

If you are new to Islam, sawm makes most sense next to the other four pillars. Our guide to the [five pillars of Islam](/blog/five-pillars-of-islam) puts it in context.

## Summary

- **Sawm is fasting in Islam:** abstaining from food, drink, and intimacy from true dawn until sunset, with the intention of worship.
- **Ramadan fasting is obligatory**, the fourth pillar of Islam ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:183](https://quran.com/2/183)).
- **Its purpose is taqwa**, awareness of Allah, not hunger for its own sake.
- **The sick, travellers, and menstruating women** make up missed days. Those permanently unable pay fidyah.
- **Eating by mistake does not break the fast.** Deliberate eating, drinking, or intercourse does.
- **Voluntary fasts** like 'Arafah, 'Ashura, Mondays, and six days of Shawwal carry extra reward.

Fasting is the one act of worship Allah says belongs to Him. A day of sawm is small and quiet. Done sincerely, it is one of the most valuable things you can give.

And Allah knows best. (والله أعلم)
