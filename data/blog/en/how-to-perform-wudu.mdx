![How to Perform Wudu: Step-by-Step Guide](/static/images/blog/how-to-perform-wudu.webp)

**To perform wudu, begin with your intention, wash your hands, rinse your mouth and nose, wash your face and arms, wipe your head, and wash your feet.** The guide below teaches a complete practical sequence. It does not label every step beyond four Quranic actions as optional for every Muslim.

## What wudu is for

Wudu is ritual ablution for prayer. [Quran 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6) names washing the face and arms to the elbows, wiping the head, and washing the feet to the ankles. The same verse distinguishes this from the purification needed in a state of major impurity.

If your question is about full-body purification, start with the [ghusl guide](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl). If you cannot use water, the [tayammum guide](/blog/how-to-perform-tayammum) introduces a different topic; do not simply skip washing steps without checking the applicable rules.

## Wudu steps in order

1. **Make the intention in your heart.** Prepare to perform ablution for worship. Begin with Bismillah when appropriate.
2. **Wash both hands to the wrists.** Begin with the right and include the spaces between your fingers.
3. **Rinse your mouth.** Move water around the mouth and spit it out.
4. **Clean your nose gently with water.** Draw water into the nose and expel it.
5. **Wash your whole face.** Cover the face from the forehead to the chin and across its width.
6. **Wash your right arm, then your left, including the elbows.** Cover each arm fully rather than leaving a dry patch.
7. **Wipe your head with wet hands.** Move from the front toward the back, then return toward the front.
8. **Wipe your ears.** Dar al-Ifta’s practical guide includes the inside and behind the ears with wet fingers and thumbs.
9. **Wash the right foot, then the left, including the ankles.** Include the spaces between your toes.

This sequence draws on [Dar al-Ifta’s practical wudu guide](https://dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/249/how-to-perform-wudhu-ablution). The report of ‘Uthman in [Sahih al-Bukhari 159](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:159) describes the hands, face, arms and feet being washed three times, with mouth and nose cleaning and head wiping. The front-to-back-and-return head movement is described in [Sahih al-Bukhari 185](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:185).

Learn the movements carefully before trying to perform them quickly. This is a complete teaching method, not a ruling that every wash must be repeated three times for validity.

## Fard and Sunnah: why a universal four-item checklist is incomplete

The four actions named in Quran 5:6 are the starting point. They are not a complete cross-school checklist of every condition and obligation.

For example, Dar al-Ifta’s [discussion of mouth rinsing](https://dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/19105/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%80%D9%85%D8%B6%D9%85%D8%B6%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%88%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B2%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B9%D8%A9) describes it as Sunnah in wudu according to the Hanafi, Maliki and Shafi‘i schools, while giving the relied-upon Hanbali position as obligatory. The classification for ghusl is not identical to the classification for wudu.

Similarly, the Maliki requirement of rubbing washed limbs is discussed in Dar al-Ifta’s [answer on differences in purification](https://dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/19281/%D8%AD%D9%83%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%84%D9%81%D9%8A%D9%82-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D9%85%D8%B0%D9%87%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A8%D8%B9%D8%B6-%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%A9). These examples explain why this guide avoids stamping every other step “Sunnah” without qualification. For a minimum-validity checklist, learn the requirements of your school with a qualified teacher, including its treatment of order, continuity and intention.

## Do I have to say Bismillah?

Say Bismillah at the start when appropriate, as the practical guide recommends. If you forgot it, do not assume that every school declares your wudu invalid. [Dar al-Ifta’s answer about remembrance in the bathroom](https://dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7840/should-i-mention-god%E2%80%99s-name-when-i-start-ablution-in-the-bathroom) treats wudu as valid when its requirements and intention are fulfilled without spoken phrases. It advises remembering the words inwardly in that setting.

This is the institution’s cited answer, not a claim that all schools classify the opening words identically. If you follow a particular school, use its guidance for your situation.

## What to say after wudu

[Sahih Muslim 234a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:234a) records the testimony of faith after completing ablution. One transliteration of the wording is:

_Ashhadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa anna Muhammadan ‘abduhu wa rasuluh._

Its meaning is to testify to Allah’s oneness and Muhammad’s servanthood and messengership. This remembrance follows washing; it does not replace any washing step. No extra instruction to look upward is needed to follow the wording cited here.
