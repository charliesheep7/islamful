**Pray two rakahs of voluntary prayer, then make the Istikhara dua and name the decision you are considering.** Jabir reported that Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) taught this practice in [Sahih al-Bukhari 1166](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1166). You are asking Allah (SWT) for the better outcome when you cannot know what lies ahead.

## How to pray Istikhara

1. **Identify the choice.** Be specific: accepting a particular job, moving to a particular city, or considering a marriage proposal. Gather the information you need and speak with people whose judgment you trust.
2. **Prepare for prayer.** Have wudu and follow your usual requirements for salah. Our [wudu guide](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu) covers the washing sequence.
3. **Pray two voluntary rakahs.** Complete them as you normally pray a two-rakah voluntary prayer, ending with salam. These are separate from your obligatory prayer. The narration does not prescribe a particular surah for Istikhara; if you are learning the movements, see [how to pray salah](/blog/how-to-pray-salah-for-beginners).
4. **Make the dua below after the prayer.** Name your choice when asking about “this matter.” [Dar al-Ifta gives the example of specifying the person in a marriage decision](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6466/istikhara-prayer-seeking-a-choice-and-its-results). Keep the actual decision clear rather than asking for an unexplained sign.

Dar al-Ifta advises performing these voluntary rakahs outside prohibited prayer times. **Istikhara does not require bedtime.** Use [your local prayer timetable](/prayer-times) for the day's prayer schedule; it is not a complete guide to every school-specific restriction on voluntary prayer.

## The complete Istikhara dua

The Arabic below follows the wording in **Bukhari 1166**, using its phrase about the outcome of one's affairs. The report also records an alternative phrase about immediate and later affairs. Those are alternatives in the narration, not extra lines that must both be added.

**Arabic**

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْتَخِيرُكَ بِعِلْمِكَ، وَأَسْتَقْدِرُكَ بِقُدْرَتِكَ، وَأَسْأَلُكَ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ الْعَظِيمِ، فَإِنَّكَ تَقْدِرُ وَلَا أَقْدِرُ، وَتَعْلَمُ وَلَا أَعْلَمُ، وَأَنْتَ عَلَّامُ الْغُيُوبِ.

اللَّهُمَّ إِنْ كُنْتَ تَعْلَمُ أَنَّ هَذَا الْأَمْرَ خَيْرٌ لِي فِي دِينِي وَمَعَاشِي وَعَاقِبَةِ أَمْرِي، فَاقْدُرْهُ لِي وَيَسِّرْهُ لِي، ثُمَّ بَارِكْ لِي فِيهِ.

وَإِنْ كُنْتَ تَعْلَمُ أَنَّ هَذَا الْأَمْرَ شَرٌّ لِي فِي دِينِي وَمَعَاشِي وَعَاقِبَةِ أَمْرِي، فَاصْرِفْهُ عَنِّي وَاصْرِفْنِي عَنْهُ، وَاقْدُرْ لِي الْخَيْرَ حَيْثُ كَانَ، ثُمَّ أَرْضِنِي بِهِ.

**Transliteration**

_Allahumma inni astakhiruka bi-'ilmika, wa astaqdiruka bi-qudratika, wa as'aluka min fadlika al-'azim. Fa-innaka taqdiru wa la aqdiru, wa ta'lamu wa la a'lamu, wa anta 'allamu al-ghuyub._

_Allahumma in kunta ta'lamu anna hadha al-amra khayrun li fi dini wa ma'ashi wa 'aqibati amri, faqdurhu li wa yassirhu li, thumma barik li fih._

_Wa in kunta ta'lamu anna hadha al-amra sharrun li fi dini wa ma'ashi wa 'aqibati amri, fasrifhu 'anni wasrifni 'anhu, waqdur li al-khayra haythu kana, thumma ardini bih._

**English meaning — an explanatory rendering of the Arabic**

Allah, I ask You to choose what is best through Your knowledge and to grant me ability through Your power. I ask from Your immense bounty. You have power where I do not; You know what I do not; nothing hidden is beyond Your knowledge.

If You know this choice will benefit my faith, my livelihood and the outcome of my affairs, decree it for me, make it possible with ease, and place Your blessing in it.

If You know it will harm my faith, my livelihood and the outcome of my affairs, keep it away from me and keep me away from it. Decree whatever good there is for me, wherever it may be, and help me be content with it.

“This matter” is **hadha al-amr**. For example, you can specify accepting a named job when asking whether it is good for you. Our existing [dua for Istikhara page](/blog/dua-for-istikhara) provides a separate reference for the supplication.

## What to do after Istikhara

**You do not have to see a dream or feel suddenly certain.** [Dar al-Ifta's answer on recognizing the result](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7938/how-to-recognize-the-result-of-istikhara-prayer) says a dream or a feeling of pleasure is not a necessary outcome. The hadith above does not instruct you to sleep afterward, wait for a color, or decode a symbol before acting.

Keep considering the real decision. For a job offer, that means its duties, conditions and effect on your responsibilities. For marriage, it includes what you know about the person and advice from people you trust. This is practical decision-making alongside the prayer; it is not a way to certify that a particular feeling is a divine message.

The dua asks for a beneficial matter to be made easier and a harmful one to be turned away. It does not give you a test that turns every minor delay into a definite “no.” You can take a considered next step without claiming to know the unseen outcome.

If a religious condition or an unusual circumstance is unclear, describe it to a qualified teacher. Avoid services that promise a guaranteed dream, color or yes/no prediction from this prayer.
