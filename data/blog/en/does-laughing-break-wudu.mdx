![Green title card reading Does Laughing Break Wudu?](/static/images/blog/does-laughing-break-wudu.webp)

Ordinary laughter outside of prayer does not break wudu. The question only becomes a real difference of opinion when the laughter happens **during the prayer itself**.

The Hanafi school holds that loud laughter in a prayer that has bowing and prostration breaks both the prayer and wudu, under specific conditions. Darul Ifta Birmingham sets these out clearly ([Darul Ifta Birmingham, _Loud laughter invalidating wudhu_](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/?p=4479)). Other scholars tie wudu to what leaves the body, and Imam al-Bukhari gathered the hadith they rely on. Both are covered below so you can follow the position of your school.

## Quick answer

| Situation                                                              | Wudu       | Prayer         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------- |
| Laughing outside prayer                                                | Not broken | Not applicable |
| Smiling silently in prayer                                             | Not broken | Valid          |
| Laughing in prayer, audible only to you (Hanafi)                       | Not broken | Broken         |
| Loud laughter in prayer that the person beside you would hear (Hanafi) | Broken     | Broken         |

The last two rows describe the Hanafi explanation. If you follow another school, ask a scholar of that school how it treats laughter inside prayer.

## The Hanafi conditions

Darul Ifta Birmingham, quoting _Sharh al-Wiqayah_, lists four conditions that must all be present for loud laughter to have this effect:

1. The person is an adult.
2. The person is awake.
3. The laughter is loud.
4. The prayer contains ruku (bowing) and sajdah (prostration).

It then separates three levels of laughter:

- **Loud laughter:** a raised sound that the person praying next to you would normally be able to hear. With the four conditions present, both wudu and the prayer are broken.
- **Laughing:** a sound you could hear yourself, but the person next to you would not. The prayer is broken, but wudu is not.
- **Smiling:** no sound at all. This has no effect on the prayer.

Because the fourth condition requires bowing and prostration, a prayer without them, such as the funeral prayer, does not fall under this ruling as Darul Ifta Birmingham explains it. Laughter outside prayer is not covered either.

### The evidence they cite

The same answer cites a narration recorded by al-Tabarani from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari. The Prophet ﷺ was leading people in prayer when a man with poor eyesight came in and fell into a hole in the mosque. Many of those praying laughed, and the Prophet ordered those who laughed to repeat their wudu and their prayer.

## The hadith that tie wudu to what leaves the body

The other side of this question rests on hadith that describe what breaks wudu in terms of something leaving the body:

- Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is no wudu except for a sound or a smell." At-Tirmidhi called it hasan sahih ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 74](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:74)).
- The prayer of a person who has done _hadath_ is not accepted until they make wudu. When a man asked Abu Hurayrah what _hadath_ means, he answered that it is passing wind ([Sahih al-Bukhari 135](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:135)).
- A person who imagines they passed wind in prayer should not leave the prayer unless they hear a sound or notice a smell ([Sahih al-Bukhari 137](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:137); [Sahih Muslim 362](https://sunnah.com/muslim:362)).

Al-Bukhari placed one version of this last hadith under a chapter titled "Whoever considers wudu necessary only for what comes out of the two passages, front and back" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 177](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:177)). The Qur'an's own verse on wudu also names relieving oneself among the things that require purification ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). None of these texts mention laughter.

The Hanafi ruling is therefore a specific exception, based on the narration above, rather than a general rule that emotion or noise breaks wudu.

## Worked examples

**You laughed at a joke after making wudu.** Your wudu is still valid. Go ahead and pray.

**You smiled during Dhuhr when a child walked in front of you.** Nothing is affected. Carry on.

**You let out a short laugh in Maghrib that only you heard, and you follow the Hanafi school.** Your prayer is broken, but your wudu is not. Start the prayer again without renewing wudu.

**You burst out laughing during Isha loudly enough that the person beside you would hear, and you follow the Hanafi school.** Both your wudu and your prayer are broken. Make wudu again, then repeat the prayer.

If you need to redo wudu and the prayer, check how much time is left before the next prayer begins on Islamful's [prayer times](/prayer-times) page. For the steps, see [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).

## Laughing in prayer is still something to avoid

Whatever your school, laughing aloud in prayer breaks the focus and humility the prayer is meant to have. If something funny happens, lower your gaze to the place of prostration and keep going. If you lose your place, start again calmly.
