![Green title card reading Does Showering Count as Wudu?](/static/images/blog/does-showering-count-as-wudu.webp)

A proper ghusl counts as wudu. If you took a purificatory bath to remove major impurity, such as after janabah, you can pray afterwards without a separate wudu, as long as nothing that breaks wudu happened after the bath.

An everyday shower is a different case. Whether it counts depends on whether it was a complete ghusl.

## What the scholars say

Egypt's Dar al-Ifta was asked by someone who took a purificatory bath, did not make ablution, and then joined a funeral prayer. It answered that ablution is included within the purificatory bath: the intention to remove major impurity also covers minor impurity, because the lesser falls within the greater. It ruled the prayer valid ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 24065](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/24065/praying-without-making-ablution-after-taking-a-purificatory-bath)).

The same fatwa cites Aisha's report that the Prophet did not make ablution after the bath, and Ibn Umar's comment to a man who did so: "You have gone beyond what is necessary."

## The hadith evidence

Aisha described the Prophet taking a bath, then praying two rak'ahs and the dawn prayer, and said: "I do not think he performed ablution afresh after taking a bath" ([Sunan Abi Dawud 250](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:250)). Al-Albani graded it sahih.

The wudu was already built into his ghusl. When the Prophet bathed after janabah, he washed his hands, "then performed ablution like that for the prayer," and then poured water over his head and his whole body ([Sahih al-Bukhari 248](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:248)).

The Qur'an mentions both purifications in one verse: washing the face, arms, head and feet for prayer, and taking a full bath when in a state of major impurity ([Qur'an 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)).

## When a shower is a valid ghusl

A shower only counts if it meets the requirements of ghusl. In the Hanafi school, Darul Ifta Birmingham lists three obligatory acts ([Darul Ifta Birmingham fatwa 03298](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/?p=13339)):

1. Rinsing the mouth with water.
2. Putting water into the nose and blowing it out.
3. Washing the whole body.

If any of these is missing, the obligation of ghusl remains. The same answer lists Sunnah acts, including saying Bismillah, making the intention of purification and performing wudu before the ghusl.

For the full method, see [how to perform ghusl](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl).

## When you still need wudu

- **Something broke your wudu after the bath.** Using the toilet, passing wind or anything else in [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu) means you need wudu before praying.
- **It was only a quick shower.** The rulings above are about a purificatory bath. If you showered just to cool off or get clean, and especially if you skipped the mouth and nose, make wudu before you pray. If you want a ruling for your situation, ask a scholar of your school.

## A simple routine

1. Make the intention of ghusl before you start.
2. Wash your hands and any impurity, then perform wudu as in [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).
3. Rinse your mouth and nose and wash your whole body.
4. Avoid anything that breaks wudu until you have prayed.
5. Check Islamful's [prayer times](/prayer-times) so you can plan your shower before the next prayer begins.
