Yes. If you made wudu first and painted your nails afterwards, you can pray with that wudu for as long as it lasts. Putting on nail polish does not break wudu. The question only comes back when your wudu breaks: ordinary waterproof polish has to come off before you make the next one.

Both fatwa services checked for this guide, Egypt's Dar al-Ifta and the Hanafi Darul Ifta Birmingham, agree on that core point. Their details follow.

## Quick answer

| Your situation                                      | What to do                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wudu first, polish applied after, wudu still intact | Pray as normal                                                |
| Wudu broke and the polish is still on               | Remove the polish, then make wudu                             |
| Polish was already on when you made wudu            | Remove it, redo wudu and repeat the prayer                    |
| Henna or dye stain on the nails                     | No need to remove it                                          |
| Breathable or halal-labelled polish                 | Doubtful; Darul Ifta Birmingham advises against relying on it |
| On your period                                      | Wearing it is not a wudu issue until your ghusl               |

## Why polish applied after wudu is fine

Dar al-Ifta in Egypt says plainly that it is permissible for a woman to use nail polish and that it does not invalidate ablution. If the polish is the kind that stops water reaching the nail, it must be removed before ablution; if it does not, like dye and henna, it need not be removed ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 6125](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6125/can-i-pray-with-my-nail-polish-or-should-i-remove-it-for-ablution)).

Darul Ifta Birmingham frames it the same way. It says the issue with nail polish generally has to do with the validity of wudu and ghusl, because water needs to reach the limbs, and non-porous polish prevents that ([Darul Ifta Birmingham, nail polish during period](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/wearing-fake-nails-and-nail-polish-during-period/)). In other words, the polish matters at the moment you wash, not while you are already in wudu. The same service even suggests applying barrier-forming products after the obligatory parts of wudu or ghusl ([silicone products](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/silicone-products/)).

## A worked example

Dar al-Ifta was asked about exactly this pattern: a woman made wudu for Fajr, put on makeup afterwards, and later needed a new wudu for Dhuhr. The answer: anything that stops water reaching the skin must be removed before making wudu, and the same applies to nail polish ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 6144](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6144/making-ablution-while-wearing-makeup-and-nail-polish)).

So in practice:

1. Make wudu and pray.
2. Paint your nails afterwards if you want to.
3. Keep praying with the same wudu while it holds. Check [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu) if you are unsure; [sleeping](/blog/does-sleeping-break-wudu) is a common one.
4. When it breaks, remove the polish completely, then make a fresh wudu.

If you want the polish to last through more than one prayer, check the gap to the next prayer on [Prayer Times](/prayer-times) and apply it right after a fresh wudu.

## If you prayed with polish that was on before wudu

Wudu requires washing the hands up to the elbows ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)), and Darul Ifta Birmingham notes that the nails are part of the hands, so water must reach them. It says wudu is not valid with gel nails on and that they must be removed ([Darul Ifta Birmingham, applying nail polish](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/is-it-permissible-to-apply-nail-polish/)).

The hadith on a missed spot shows what follows. Umar reported that the Prophet ﷺ saw a man who had left a spot the size of a fingernail on his foot dry, and told him to repeat both his wudu and his prayer ([Sunan Ibn Majah 666](https://sunnah.com/ibnmajah:666)). This guide applies that to polish: remove it, make wudu again, and repeat the prayers you prayed with the incomplete wudu.

## Henna, dye and breathable polish

- **Henna and dye.** A colour stain is not a layer, so it does not need to come off ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 6125](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6125/can-i-pray-with-my-nail-polish-or-should-i-remove-it-for-ablution)). Darul Ifta Birmingham quotes al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya to the same effect for a woman who has dyed her fingers with henna ([wudhu with makeup](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/wudhu-with-makeup/)).
- **Breathable polish.** Darul Ifta Birmingham says there is a huge doubt and no full guarantee that water passes through the entire layer, and asks why anyone would risk their wudu, and with it their prayer, for it ([applying nail polish](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/is-it-permissible-to-apply-nail-polish/)).
- **Thick versus thin films.** Its general test is whether the barrier is thick, visible and clearly stops water reaching the skin. Nail polish is its example of a barrier that does; oil and Vaseline are examples of films that do not ([silicone products](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/silicone-products/)). For makeup and other products, see [can you do wudu with makeup on](/blog/can-you-do-wudu-with-makeup-on).

## During your period

Darul Ifta Birmingham says nail polish and fake nails are permissible to wear during your period as long as they contain nothing impure, because you are not required to make wudu or ghusl in that time ([nail polish during period](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/wearing-fake-nails-and-nail-polish-during-period/)). Since the same answer says water must reach the limbs for ghusl, take the polish off before your ghusl when the period ends. The steps are in [how to perform ghusl](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl).

This guide checked two fatwa services and did not find a checked source that allows wudu over ordinary waterproof polish. If you follow a particular teacher or madhhab, confirm the finer details with them.
