![Dark green title card reading Can You Do Wudu Over Shoes?](/static/images/blog/can-you-do-wudu-over-shoes.webp)

For ordinary shoes and trainers, the majority answer is no: take them off and wash your feet. Darul Ifta Birmingham says this directly and attributes it to the majority of scholars. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta gives a wider allowance for shoes that cover most of the foot, so this is a real disagreement, not a settled rule.

Leather footwear that meets the conditions of a khuff is different. Wiping over that kind of footwear is agreed on. If you only want to know about socks, see [can you do wudu with socks on](/blog/can-you-do-wudu-with-socks-on).

## Quick answer

| What you are wearing                             | Darul Ifta Birmingham                                  | Dar al-Ifta (Egypt)                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ordinary shoes or trainers                       | Not permissible to wipe                                | Allowed if they cover most of the foot and were put on with wudu          |
| Leather boots or khuffs that meet the conditions | Permissible                                            | Permissible                                                               |
| Open sandals, slip-ons or low shoes              | Do not meet the condition of covering up to the ankles | Not covered: the allowance is only for footwear covering most of the foot |
| Bare feet                                        | Wash them                                              | Wash them                                                                 |

## Why the default is washing your feet

The verse on wudu commands washing the feet to the ankles ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). Wiping is an allowance taken from the Prophet's practice ﷺ. Dar al-Ifta makes the same point about gloves and sleeves: worship is not settled by logical comparison, and wiping applies to socks and shoes only ([Dar al-Ifta, fatwa 7844](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7844/why-women-are-obliged-to-remove-their-nail-polish-in-ablution-while-they-are-all)).

The clearest evidence concerns khuffs. Al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah poured water for the Prophet ﷺ on a journey, and he washed his face and forearms and wiped his head and his two khuffs ([Sahih al-Bukhari 182](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:182)). On another journey, al-Mughirah went to take the khuffs off. The Prophet ﷺ told him to leave them, as he had put them on after ablution, and wiped over them ([Sahih al-Bukhari 206](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:206)).

Darul Ifta Birmingham records scholarly consensus on wiping over soft leather boots, at home or travelling, and says that consensus does not extend to footwear of other materials ([fatwa 03316](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/masah-on-cotton-socks/)).

## Where the scholars differ on ordinary shoes

**Most scholars: no.** Darul Ifta Birmingham, citing Maarif al-Sunan, says it is not permissible to wipe over shoes or trainers and that this is the majority view ([Masah over normal socks](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/masah-over-normal-socks/)). It explains the reports of the Prophet ﷺ wiping over his shoes this way: he did so when he was already in wudu and was renewing it for a prayer, not when his wudu had broken. It cites a report from Ali, in Sahih Ibn Khuzaymah, saying exactly that. We read that explanation in the Birmingham answer and did not check Ibn Khuzaymah directly.

**A wider allowance.** Egypt's Dar al-Ifta says that if you put on socks, shoes or sandals, only those covering most of the foot, while you have valid wudu, you need not take them off each time. You can wipe the top of each covered foot once with wet hands, for up to 24 hours, or three days when travelling ([How to perform wudhu](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/249/how-to-perform-wudhu-ablution)).

**The disputed hadith.** Al-Mughirah also reported that the Prophet ﷺ wiped over his socks and sandals ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 99](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:99)). At-Tirmidhi graded it hasan sahih, while Darussalam's grade on the same page is da'if. Darul Ifta Birmingham relates that imams including Muslim, al-Bayhaqi and Ahmad considered it weak.

The practical upshot: **if you wipe over ordinary shoes, your wudu is valid on the Dar al-Ifta view and invalid on the majority view.** Washing your feet is valid on both, so it is the safe choice whenever you can do it.

## When footwear counts as a khuff

Darul Ifta Birmingham gives four conditions for leather footwear, in an answer on overshoes ([fatwa on the jarmuq](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/the-definition-of-jarmuq-and-the-ruling-on-wiping-over-it/)):

1. It covers the area washed in wudu, up to the ankles.
2. You can walk a distance in it without it tearing.
3. It stops water seeping through easily.
4. It was put on in a state of complete purity.

So a laced leather ankle boot put on after wudu may qualify. A low trainer or an open sandal fails the first condition, because part of the washed area is exposed. If you are unsure about a particular pair, ask a scholar of your school or wash your feet.

## How long wiping lasts, and how to do it

Safwan ibn Assal reported that when travelling they were ordered not to remove their khuffs for three days and nights, except for janabah ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 96](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:96)). Dar al-Ifta's note gives the same three days for travellers and 24 hours otherwise.

Wipe the top of the footwear, not the sole. Ali ibn Abi Talib said that if religion were based on opinion, the underside would matter more, but he saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ wipe the upper part ([Sunan Abi Dawud 162](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:162), graded sahih by al-Albani). You must take your footwear off and do a full [ghusl](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl) after janabah.

## A worked example

You are at work in trainers and need wudu for Dhuhr. The trainers stop below the ankle, so the majority view does not allow wiping over them.

1. Do the steps of [wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu) as usual.
2. Take off your trainers and socks and wash your feet to the ankles, for example in a sink or with a bottle of water over a drain.
3. Put them back on.

If you put on thick socks after wudu that morning, you may have a simpler option. Take off the trainers only and wipe over the socks, as long as they meet the conditions in [our socks guide](/blog/can-you-do-wudu-with-socks-on). Thin socks are not accepted by most scholars.

If you are outdoors and truly have no water, the question changes: see [how to perform tayammum](/blog/how-to-perform-tayammum).

## Wiping over shoes and praying in shoes are different questions

Wiping is about wudu. Wearing shoes during prayer is a separate matter. The Prophet ﷺ did pray in his shoes ([Sahih al-Bukhari 386](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:386)), and he told people to check their sandals for filth at the mosque, wipe it off and pray in them ([Sunan Abi Dawud 650](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:650), graded sahih by al-Albani). Dar al-Ifta says that with today's carpeted mosques it is better not to pray in shoes, unless you have a legitimate difficulty removing them and they are clean ([fatwa 8355](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/8355/praying-in-shoes-inside-mosques)).

## Your next step

The easiest way to avoid washing your feet at work is to make wudu at home before putting on thick socks or leather boots. Check today's [prayer times](/prayer-times) so you can see which prayers fall in your working day and plan where you will make wudu.
