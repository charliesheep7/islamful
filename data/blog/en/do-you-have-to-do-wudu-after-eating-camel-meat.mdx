It depends on which school of Islamic law you follow. In the Hanbali school, eating camel meat breaks wudu, so you make fresh wudu before your next prayer. In the Hanafi, Maliki and Shafi'i schools it does not, and your wudu stays valid.

Both positions come from authentic evidence, which is why the question keeps coming up. This guide sets out the hadith, what each side says about them, and what to do in practice. It relies on fatwas from Egypt's Dar al-Ifta and Darul Ifta Birmingham (Hanafi); it is not a personal ruling for your situation.

## Quick answer

| If you follow            | Does camel meat break wudu? | Before your next prayer                                                   |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hanbali school           | Yes, raw or cooked          | Make fresh wudu                                                           |
| Hanafi school            | No                          | Wudu is still valid; rinsing the mouth is enough                          |
| Maliki or Shafi'i school | No                          | Wudu is still valid                                                       |
| Not sure                 | Scholars differ             | Fresh wudu satisfies both views; ask your local imam which view to follow |

## The hadith behind the question

Two hadith specifically mention wudu after camel meat.

In [Sahih Muslim 360a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:360a), Jabir ibn Samura reports that a man asked the Prophet whether he should perform ablution after eating mutton. The Prophet said he could if he wished, or not if he did not. The man then asked about camel meat, and the Prophet said: "Yes, perform ablution (after eating) camel's flesh."

In [Jami at-Tirmidhi 81](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:81), al-Bara ibn Azib reports the same distinction: the Prophet told people to perform wudu for camel meat and not to perform it for goat meat. Sunnah.com lists this hadith as sahih. In his comments on the hadith, at-Tirmidhi notes that requiring wudu was the position of Ahmad and Ishaq, while Sufyan al-Thawri and the scholars of Kufa did not require it.

## View 1: camel meat breaks wudu (Hanbali)

Egypt's Dar al-Ifta summarises the Hanbali position this way: the wudu of a person who eats camel meat, raw or cooked, is nullified, whether or not they knew what kind of meat it was ([Dar al-Ifta, fatwa 7826](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7826/does-camel-meat-break-ablution)). Their evidence is the plain wording of the hadith of al-Bara.

If you follow this view, eating camel meat is like any other thing that breaks wudu. You do not need to make wudu straight after the meal, but you must have fresh wudu before you pray or do anything else that requires it.

## View 2: camel meat does not break wudu (majority)

The same Dar al-Ifta fatwa lists al-Thawri, Imam Malik, al-Shafi'i and ahl al-ra'y among the scholars who held that camel meat does not nullify ablution. A related Dar al-Ifta fatwa explains that ahl al-ra'y refers to scholars such as Abu Hanifah and his students ([Dar al-Ifta, fatwa 6239](https://dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6239/camel-meat-and-ablution)).

These scholars rely on a report from Jabir that "the last of the two commands" from the Prophet was to stop making wudu for eating anything cooked over fire. Dar al-Ifta notes that the majority of scholars considered the hadith of al-Bara and similar reports to have been abrogated by Jabir's report. In fatwa 6239, Dar al-Ifta states that the opinion it chooses for fatwa is that eating camel meat does not nullify ablution.

The Hanafi school offers a second explanation. Darul Ifta Birmingham says wudu does not break, so you do not need to make wudu, and that the hadith used the literal meaning of wudu, which is washing the mouth, "since camel meat is very fatty and thick" ([Darul Ifta Birmingham, fatwa 02783](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/?p=11161)).

## What to do in practice

1. **Follow your school.** If you follow a madhhab or a local scholar, use their ruling. Both views are held by recognised schools.
2. **If you follow the Hanbali view,** make fresh wudu before your next prayer, using the normal [steps of wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).
3. **If you follow the Hanafi, Maliki or Shafi'i view,** your wudu is still valid. Rinsing your mouth and washing your hands after a fatty meal is good manners, but it is not a new wudu.
4. **If you are unsure,** making fresh wudu before praying means your wudu is valid under both views. That is this guide's practical observation, not a ruling that wudu is obligatory.

A worked example: you have wudu, eat a camel burger at lunch, and Asr is in an hour. Under the Hanbali view you renew your wudu before Asr. Under the other three schools you can pray Asr with the wudu you already had. Check the exact time on our [prayer times tool](/prayer-times) so you know how long you have.

The fatwas checked here discuss eating camel meat. They do not address camel milk, broth or other camel products, so ask a scholar about those rather than assuming the same ruling applies.
