Yes. You can do dhikr and make dua without wudu. Saying SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, La ilaha illallah and Allahu Akbar, sending salawat on the Prophet ﷺ and asking Allah for what you need are all valid without wudu. Being in wudu is better when you can manage it, but it is not a condition.

What does need wudu is salah. Reading from a physical mushaf is treated separately again. The sections below show the evidence, the one hadith that seems to point the other way, and what changes during menstruation or janabah.

## Quick answer

| What you want to do                   | Without wudu?                                                          |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dhikr: tasbih, tahmid, takbir, tahlil | Yes                                                                    |
| Dua for anything you need             | Yes                                                                    |
| Salawat on the Prophet ﷺ              | Yes                                                                    |
| Reciting Quran from memory            | Yes, according to the scholars al-Tirmidhi names, unless you are junub |
| Reading from a mushaf                 | Those same scholars say only while pure                                |
| Salah                                 | No, wudu is required                                                   |

## Why dhikr and dua do not need wudu

The Quran praises “those who remember Allah while standing, sitting, and lying on their sides” ([Surah Al Imran 3:191](https://quran.com/3/191)) and tells the believers to remember Allah often ([Surah Al-Ahzab 33:41](https://quran.com/33/41)). Neither verse makes remembrance depend on purity. Wudu, by contrast, is tied to prayer: the wudu verse opens with “When you rise up for prayer” ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)).

The clearest practical evidence comes from Eid. Umm Atiyya reported that women, including menstruating women, were told to come out on the day of Eid, stand behind the men, say takbir with them and invoke Allah with them ([Sahih al-Bukhari 971](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:971)). A woman on her period cannot pray, yet she was included in both the takbir and the dua. This guide draws the plain conclusion: if dhikr and dua are open to her, they are open to someone who has simply lost their wudu.

The Quran itself carries more weight than general dhikr, and even there the bar is low. Ali reported that the Prophet ﷺ recited the Quran in every condition as long as he was not junub. Imam al-Tirmidhi adds that many Companions and later scholars held that a person may recite the Quran without wudu but should read from the mushaf only while pure, and he names Sufyan al-Thawri, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad and Ishaq among them ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 146](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:146)). Dhikr and dua are not Quran recitation, so this guide treats them as the lighter case.

## The hadith that seems to say otherwise

Muhajir ibn Qunfudh greeted the Prophet ﷺ while he was urinating. The Prophet ﷺ did not reply until he had made wudu, then apologised and said he disliked remembering Allah except in a state of purity ([Sunan Abi Dawud 17](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:17)).

The word in the narration is dislike, not prohibition, and it describes the Prophet's own preference. Read alongside the Eid report and Tirmidhi 146, this guide takes it to show that wudu is the better state for remembrance, not that dhikr without it is invalid.

## When wudu is recommended

Some Sunnah practices pair wudu with dhikr and dua, which is why making wudu first is a good habit when it is easy:

- **Before sleep.** The Prophet ﷺ told al-Bara ibn Azib to make wudu as for prayer, lie on his right side, and then say the bedtime dua beginning Allahumma aslamtu wajhi ilayk ([Sahih al-Bukhari 247](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:247)). The full wording is in [dua for sleeping](/blog/dua-for-sleeping).
- **Before an important dua.** When Abu Musa brought him Abu Amir's request for forgiveness, the Prophet ﷺ asked for water, made wudu, raised his hands and made dua for him ([Sahih al-Bukhari 4323](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4323)).

These show what is best, not what is required. If you are on a bus, at work or lying in bed without wudu, do not hold back your dhikr or dua while you wait for water.

## During menstruation, postnatal bleeding or janabah

Dhikr and dua stay open in these states too. The difference is Quran recitation.

Darul Ifta Birmingham, a Hanafi fatwa service, says a woman on her period may read durud and other dhikr, and may recite Quranic verses with the intention of dua, though not with the intention of recitation ([fatwa 02055](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/can-a-woman-in-her-periods-recite-durud-shareef-2/)). In another answer it lists Surah al-Ikhlas, al-Falaq, an-Nas and Ayat al-Kursi as passages she may read as dua or dhikr, and advises her to hold off on Surah al-Kahf, al-Mulk and al-Kafirun because they do not carry the meaning of supplication or dhikr ([fatwa 05270](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/reciting-quran-whilst-menstruating/)).

That is the Hanafi position from one fatwa service. Other schools have their own details on recitation during menstruation, and this guide did not check them, so ask a teacher you trust if you follow another madhhab.

## A routine that works with or without wudu

1. Keep a short dhikr going through the day: SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, La ilaha illallah, Allahu Akbar. The [Dhikr Counter](/dhikr) helps if you like to keep count.
2. Say the morning, evening and after-prayer remembrances from [Daily Adhkar](/adhkar). Quranic duas in them can be said as dua; just keep the menstruation details above in mind if they apply to you.
3. Ask Allah for your needs at any time. The [Dua Collection](/dua) has wording for common situations, and [how to make dua that gets accepted](/blog/how-to-make-dua-that-gets-accepted) covers the etiquette.
4. When salah is due, make wudu then. Check the time on [Prayer Times](/prayer-times), and see [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu) if you are unsure whether yours is still valid.

For reading the Quran itself, see [can you read Quran without wudu](/blog/can-you-read-quran-without-wudu).
