![Dark green illustration with the title Does Discharge Break Wudu](/static/images/blog/does-discharge-break-wudu.webp)

Discharge is one of the most common wudu questions, and the honest answer depends on which fluid you mean. Some fluids break wudu by agreement, one requires a full bath, and ordinary vaginal discharge is a point where scholars differ. This guide separates them so you can act with confidence and stop second-guessing every prayer.

## The short answer

- **Madhi** (clear, sticky fluid released with arousal) breaks wudu. It does not require ghusl.
- **Wadi** (thick white fluid around urination) breaks wudu.
- **Mani** (semen or sexual fluid released with climax) requires ghusl, not just wudu.
- **Ordinary vaginal discharge** is disputed. One Hanafi fatwa says clear, normal wetness is pure and does not break wudu; Egypt's Dar al-Ifta says vaginal secretions do break it. Follow your school or a scholar you trust.
- **Constant discharge** you cannot stop long enough to pray makes you excused: you make wudu once per prayer time and pray.

## Madhi and wadi: wudu, not ghusl

Ali ibn Abi Talib had frequent madhi and asked the Prophet ﷺ about it through al-Miqdad. The answer was that he should wash his private part and perform ablution ([Sahih Muslim 303a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:303a)). Nothing more than wudu was required.

Darul Ifta Birmingham, a Hanafi institution, describes three fluids. Madhi is a thin clear liquid released with arousal and wadi is a thick white fluid released before or after urinating; both require wudu. Mani, released at climax, requires ghusl ([Mazur With Regular Discharge](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/mazur-with-regular-discharge/)). Another of its answers confirms that a clear sticky discharge is madhi, that it breaks wudu without requiring ghusl, and that the soiled part of clothing should be washed ([Discharging of Madhi](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/discharging-of-madhi/)).

If you are unsure whether you need a full bath, see [how to perform ghusl](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl).

## Normal vaginal discharge: where scholars differ

**The view that it does not break wudu.** Mufti Mohammed Tosir Miah of Darul Ifta Birmingham explains that the normal, regular wetness a woman feels, which is clear and not discoloured, is called rutubat al-farj. He rules it pure, so wudu does not break, citing Radd al-Muhtar ([Does Vaginal Discharge Break the Wudhu](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/does-vaginal-discharge-break-the-wudhu/)). The same answer adds that if a woman uses a pad, wudu is not broken until discharge shows on the pad outside the inner part of the body.

**The view that it does break wudu.** Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyyah (Egypt) answered a woman asking about irregular vaginal secretions not caused by illness or arousal. Its answer, given by Sheikh Hassouna al-Nawawi, is that such secretions do break wudu but do not require ghusl. She washes the area, cleans or changes her underwear if it is wet, and then makes wudu again ([Dar al-Ifta, fatwa 13044](https://www.dar-alifta.org/fr/fatwa/details/13044/les-secretions-vaginales-annulent-elles-les-ablutions)).

Both positions come from qualified institutions. Neither is a fringe opinion, and neither asks you to bathe after normal discharge. Pick the view of the school you follow and apply it consistently instead of switching each time doubt appears.

## If discharge is constant: the excused person

Fatimah bint Abi Hubaysh had continuous bleeding outside her period. The Prophet ﷺ told her to keep praying and, in the narration of Hisham's father, to perform ablution for every prayer until her period returned ([Sahih al-Bukhari 228](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:228)). Scholars apply the same principle to other constant flows.

Darul Ifta Birmingham explains that if discharge continues so that you cannot make wudu and pray the obligatory rak'ahs without it, you are excused (ma'dhur). You make wudu for each obligatory prayer, and it remains valid for that prayer's time; other nullifiers still break it ([Does Vaginal Discharge Break the Wudhu](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/does-vaginal-discharge-break-the-wudhu/)). Dar al-Ifta in Egypt gives the same outcome for regular, constant secretions, treating them like urinary incontinence, and says constantly wet underwear need not be washed while the excuse lasts ([Dar al-Ifta, fatwa 13044](https://www.dar-alifta.org/fr/fatwa/details/13044/les-secretions-vaginales-annulent-elles-les-ablutions)).

Being excused for one fluid does not cover another. A woman excused for habitual discharge who then has madhi must still purify herself and make wudu before praying ([Does Discharge Break the Wudhu?](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/does-discharge-break-the-wudhu/)).

Because an excused person's wudu is tied to the prayer time, it helps to know exactly when each time starts and ends. Check today's times on the [Islamful prayer times tool](/prayer-times).

## A practical routine

1. **Identify the fluid.** Arousal fluid (madhi) or fluid around urination (wadi) means wudu. Fluid with climax means ghusl.
2. **Wash the area** and any affected part of your clothing.
3. **Make wudu**, following the steps in [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).
4. **If the flow is constant**, make wudu after the prayer time begins and pray that time's prayer with it.
5. **Use a liner or tissue** to keep clothing clean. Darul Ifta Birmingham suggests this and also advises seeing a doctor if heavy discharge persists ([Wudu and madhi](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/wudu-and-madhi/)).

## Dealing with doubt

Many people, especially those prone to waswas, start checking constantly. Darul Ifta Birmingham's advice to a questioner with OCD-like doubts was not to dwell on it, because the more you think about it, the more it grows, and to build a habit of remembering Allah ([Mazur With Regular Discharge](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/mazur-with-regular-discharge/)). If you did not see or feel anything leave, carry on.

## When to see a doctor

The NHS describes normal vaginal discharge as clear or white, slippery or sticky, and without a strong smell. A change in colour, smell or texture, itching, or bleeding between periods can signal an infection and is a reason to get medical advice ([NHS: Vaginal discharge](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vaginal-discharge/)). A doctor treats the cause; the fiqh questions above stay the same.
