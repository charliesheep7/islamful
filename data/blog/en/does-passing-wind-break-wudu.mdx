![Dark green title card reading Does Passing Wind Break Wudu?](/static/images/blog/does-passing-wind-break-wudu.webp)

Yes. Passing wind from the back passage breaks wudu, whether it is loud or silent. Before your next prayer you need to perform wudu again.

The important detail is certainty. A real release of wind breaks wudu. A passing feeling or a doubt does not.

## The evidence

The Qur'an lists coming from relieving oneself among the things that require purification before prayer ([Qur'an 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). The Sunnah names wind specifically. Abu Hurairah reported that the Messenger of Allah said: "There is no Wudu except for a sound or a smell" ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 74](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:74)). Tirmidhi graded it hasan sahih.

Darul Ifta Birmingham, a Hanafi institute, summarises the ruling: anything that exits from the front or back passage nullifies wudu, with one exception for women covered below. It also quotes the hadith that the prayer of a person who breaks wind is not accepted until they perform wudu ([Darul Ifta Birmingham](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/?p=976)).

## Certainty versus doubt

Many people worry because they feel movement in the stomach during prayer. The Prophet addressed exactly this. Abbad bin Tamim's uncle asked about a man who imagined he had passed wind during prayer, and he replied that he should not leave the prayer "unless he hears sound or smells something" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 137](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:137); also [Sahih al-Bukhari 177](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:177)).

A similar narration says that if someone in the masjid feels wind between his buttocks, he should not go out until he hears a sound or notices a smell ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 75](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:75)). Tirmidhi notes that the scholars hold wudu is only required for a hadath that is heard or smelled, and records Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak's view that doubt does not require wudu until one is certain.

Sound and smell are signs of certainty, not extra conditions. Darul Ifta Birmingham explains that if you are certain you passed even a small amount of gas, your wudu breaks even without sound or smell. If you are not certain, your wudu stays intact.

In practice:

- **You know wind came out:** wudu is broken.
- **You only felt a rumble or pressure:** wudu stands. Carry on.
- **You keep second-guessing:** act on what you are sure of. Repeated doubt is not a reason to keep redoing wudu.

## If it happens during prayer

Because prayer is not accepted without wudu, a person who is sure they passed wind should leave the prayer, perform wudu and pray again. A narration in Sunan Abi Dawud states this directly, but al-Albani graded it weak ([Sunan Abi Dawud 205](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:205)), so the ruling rests on the general evidence above rather than on that narration.

In congregation, step out quietly, renew your wudu and rejoin or pray on your own.

## Wind from the front passage

Women sometimes notice air leaving the front passage. In the Hanafi school this does not break wudu: Darul Ifta Birmingham, citing Raddul Muhtar, excludes air from the front passage of a female from the things that nullify wudu. If you follow another school, ask a scholar of that school. Vaginal discharge is a separate question, covered in [does discharge break wudu](/blog/does-discharge-break-wudu).

If wind or anything else leaves you continuously because of a medical condition, ask a qualified scholar about the rulings for an ongoing excuse rather than repeating wudu endlessly.
