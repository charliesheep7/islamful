Maybe it started with one missed Fajr. Then a busy season, then guilt, then months where praying felt like something other people did. If you are reading this, part of you wants to come back. That wish is where the return begins.

> **Quick answer:** To start praying again, make sincere _tawbah_ (repentance), relearn wudu and the steps of salah, and pray the very next prayer that is due. Build up with small daily anchors instead of waiting to feel ready. Allah says, "Do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins" (Surah Az-Zumar, 39:53).

**What you'll learn:**

- Why it is never too late to come back to salah
- A practical, step-by-step plan for your first weeks back
- What scholars say about the prayers you missed

## Why is it never too late to return to prayer?

Salah is the most important act of worship after faith itself. The Prophet ﷺ said the first deed a person will be called to account for on the Day of Resurrection is their prayer (Narrated by Tirmidhi, 413). He also warned that "between a man and shirk and disbelief is abandoning the prayer" (Narrated by Muslim, 82).

That is serious. But the Quran pairs the warning with an open door. After describing people who "neglected prayer", Allah adds "except those who repent, believe and do righteousness" ([Surah Maryam, 19:59–60](https://quran.com/19/59-60)).

And to everyone who has wronged themselves, Allah says in [Surah Az-Zumar, 39:53](https://quran.com/39/53):

> قُلْ يَا عِبَادِيَ الَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا مِن رَّحْمَةِ اللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا
>
> _Qul ya 'ibadiyal-ladhina asrafu 'ala anfusihim la taqnatu min rahmatillah. Innallaha yaghfirudh-dhunuba jami'a_
>
> "Say, O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins."

In a hadith qudsi, Allah says that if His servant comes to Him walking, He comes to him running (Narrated by Bukhari, 7405). Your first step back is met with far more than you give.

## Before you begin

You do not need to fix your whole life before your first prayer. You need three things:

- **Sincere intention.** Returning for Allah, not to impress anyone.
- **Basic knowledge of wudu and salah.** It is normal to have forgotten. Refresh with our guides on [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu) and [how to pray salah for beginners](/blog/how-to-pray-salah-for-beginners).
- **Today's prayer times.** You cannot pray on time if you do not know the time. Check them below.

## Step-by-step: how to start praying again

### Step 1: Make tawbah right now

Scholars list the conditions of sincere repentance as regretting the sin, stopping it, and resolving not to return to it ([IslamQA](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/610/repentance-for-neglecting-prayer)). You do not need a special ritual. Speak to Allah in your own words, and seek forgiveness:

> أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ وَأَتُوبُ إِلَيْهِ
>
> _Astaghfirullaha wa atubu ilayh_
>
> "I seek Allah's forgiveness and I repent to Him."

The Prophet ﷺ said, "Every son of Adam sins, and the best of sinners are those who repent" (Narrated by Tirmidhi, 2499). For more supplications, see our [dua for forgiveness](/blog/dua-for-forgiveness).

### Step 2: Pray the next prayer that is due

Do not wait for Monday, Ramadan, or "when things calm down". Look at the clock, find the next prayer, make wudu, and pray it.

**Common mistake:** planning a perfect restart and never beginning. One imperfect prayer today is worth more than a flawless plan for next month.

### Step 3: Anchor each prayer to something you already do

Habits stick when they attach to existing routines:

- **Fajr** — set an alarm a few minutes before the adhan and keep your prayer mat by the bed. Our [Fajr guide](/blog/how-to-pray-fajr) covers the details.
- **Dhuhr** — pray at the start of your lunch break.
- **Asr** — pray before leaving work or school.
- **Maghrib** — pray as soon as you get home, before dinner.
- **Isha** — pray before your evening screen time, not after.

### Step 4: If five feels impossible, never drop below what you have

All five daily prayers are obligatory, and the goal is all five on time. But if you are rebuilding from nothing, protect the prayers you manage and add the next one quickly. Never let a missed prayer become an excuse to miss the one after it.

The Prophet ﷺ said, "The most beloved deed to Allah is the most regular and constant, even if it is little" (Narrated by Bukhari, 6464).

### Step 5: Relearn what you say in prayer

Prayer feels empty when the words feel foreign. Learn the meaning of Surah Al-Fatihah first, then the short surahs you recite. Knowing what you are saying turns salah from a routine into a conversation.

Allah reminds us that prayer is a source of strength ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:45](https://quran.com/2/45)):

> وَاسْتَعِينُوا بِالصَّبْرِ وَالصَّلَاةِ
>
> _Wasta'inu bis-sabri was-salah_
>
> "And seek help through patience and prayer."

### Step 6: Make dua to stay consistent

Ask Allah to keep you steady, using the dua of Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him) from Surah Ibrahim, 14:40:

> رَبِّ اجْعَلْنِي مُقِيمَ الصَّلَاةِ وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِي
>
> _Rabbij-'alni muqimas-salati wa min dhurriyyati_
>
> "My Lord, make me an establisher of prayer, and from my descendants."

## What about the prayers you missed?

This is where scholars differ, and it is worth knowing both views. Our full guide on [how to make up missed prayers](/blog/how-to-make-up-missed-prayers) walks through the method.

| Situation | Scholarly view | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prayers missed after puberty (including deliberately) | Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools: must be made up | Estimate the number and pray them gradually, e.g. one extra set with each daily prayer |
| Prayers deliberately abandoned | Minority view (Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Hazm): cannot be made up | Sincere repentance plus many voluntary prayers and good deeds |
| Prayers missed before puberty | Agreed: no make-up required | Nothing to make up |
| Prayers missed before accepting Islam | Agreed: no make-up required | Nothing to make up |

The majority position is the safer one to follow. Whichever view you follow, **do not let the backlog stop you from praying today's prayers on time.** Today's prayer always comes first.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Waiting to feel spiritual first.** Feelings often follow action. Pray, and let the heart catch up.
- **Going all-or-nothing.** Missing one prayer does not undo your return. Pray the next one.
- **Letting shame keep you away.** Allah forbids despairing of His mercy (39:53). Shame that pushes you away from prayer is not from Him.
- **Praying without wudu or with rushed movements.** Take the time to do wudu properly and to be still in each position.
- **Doing it alone.** Find a mosque, a friend, or a class. Company makes consistency far easier.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Will my prayers be accepted if I still commit other sins?**
Yes, keep praying. Allah describes prayer as something that "prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Surah Al-Ankabut, 29:45). Prayer is part of the cure, not a reward for already being cured.

**Q: I have forgotten most of what to recite. Should I wait until I relearn it?**
No. Pray with what you know while you relearn. Make Surah Al-Fatihah your first priority, because the Prophet ﷺ said, "There is no prayer for the one who does not recite Al-Fatihah" (Narrated by Bukhari, 756).

**Q: Should I tell people I stopped praying?**
No. Repentance is between you and Allah. You do not need to announce past sins.

## Summary

- Repent sincerely: regret, stop, and resolve not to return.
- Refresh wudu and salah, then pray the **next prayer that is due**.
- Anchor each prayer to your daily routine and keep it consistent.
- Follow a scholarly method for missed prayers without letting it stop today's prayers.
- If you slip, return immediately. Allah comes running to the one who walks toward Him.

Allah knows best.
