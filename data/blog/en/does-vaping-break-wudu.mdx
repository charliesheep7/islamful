![Warm sand coloured illustration with the title Does Vaping Break Wudu](/static/images/blog/does-vaping-break-wudu.webp)

Smoking and vaping are not on the list of things that break wudu. The scholarly sources reviewed here say smoking does not invalidate ablution, and none of them treats vaping as a nullifier. That is a separate question from whether vaping is permitted at all, which scholars answer differently and mostly negatively. This guide keeps the two apart.

## The short answer

- **Does smoking break wudu?** No. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta states that smoking does not invalidate ablution, and Darul Ifta Birmingham gives the same answer.
- **Does vaping break wudu?** The sources reviewed do not name vaping as a nullifier. Vaping is not an exit from the front or back passage, and it is not one of the causes named in the texts.
- **Is it fine to vape or smoke?** That is a different question. Rulings on harm are covered below and in our guides on [is vaping haram](/blog/is-vaping-haram) and [is smoking haram](/blog/is-smoking-haram).

## What actually breaks wudu

The Prophet ﷺ said that the prayer of one who has broken wudu is not accepted until he performs ablution ([Sahih Muslim 225](https://sunnah.com/muslim:225)). When Abu Hurairah was asked what hadath is, he explained it as passing wind ([Sahih al-Bukhari 176](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:176)). Wudu is broken by recognised causes, such as things leaving the front or back passage and deep sleep. Inhaling smoke or vapour is not among them. For the wider list, read [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu).

## What scholars say about smoking and wudu

Egypt's Dar al-Ifta answers that smoking does not invalidate ablution, adding that it is recommended to cleanse the mouth of the odour of smoke before prayer so as not to offend fellow worshippers ([Does smoking invalidate ablution?](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/22372/does-smoking-invalidate-wu%E1%B8%8Du%CA%BE)).

Darul Ifta Birmingham gives the same ruling and says it is preferable to clean the mouth thoroughly before prayer or recitation of the Quran ([Smoking break wudhu](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/smoking-break-wudhu/)).

## What this means for vaping

None of the sources reviewed rules on vaping and wudu directly. Reasoning from the smoking answers, vaping is not an exit from the passages and not a named nullifier, so it does not by itself break wudu. That is an inference from the sources above, not a quoted ruling, so ask a scholar if you want a ruling for your school.

Practical point: if you vape before prayer, rinse your mouth, as the smoking fatwas recommend for odour.

## A separate question: is vaping permitted?

Whether wudu stays valid is not the same as whether the act is allowed.

- Darul Ifta Birmingham says vaping would not fall under the prohibitions of ihram, but is not advised, and is similar to smoking, which scholars have deemed haram or at least makruh tahrimi, very close to haram ([Vaping During Ihraam](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/vaping-during-ihraam/)).
- The Fatwa Committee of MUIS in Singapore rules the use of all types of vape and Kpod to be haram, in line with the ruling on cigarettes, based on the principle against what causes harm and the aim of protecting life and mind ([MUIS fatwa on vape](https://www.muis.gov.sg/resources/khutbah-and-religious-advice/fatwa/hukum-menggunakan-vape/)).

Vaping and fasting is a third question with its own answer; see [does vaping break your fast](/blog/does-vaping-break-your-fast).

## What to do next

1. Keep your wudu if you only smoked or vaped; renew it only if a recognised nullifier occurred, following [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).
2. Rinse your mouth before prayer.
3. Check the next prayer time with the [prayer times](/prayer-times) tool.
4. If you want to quit, speak to your doctor and see the harm guidance in the linked guides.
