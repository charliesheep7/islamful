Tajweed is how the Quran was recited to the Prophet ﷺ, and how he recited it to his Companions. It is not decoration. It is the difference between reading Arabic letters and reciting the words of Allah (SWT) the way they were revealed.

> **Quick answer:** To recite with tajweed, you learn where each letter is pronounced (_makharij_), then apply a small set of rules: how noon and meem sakinah blend into the next letter, the bouncing letters of _qalqalah_, the stretched vowels of _madd_, and the stopping signs. Allah commands, "Recite the Quran with measured recitation" (Surah Al-Muzzammil, 73:4).

**What you'll learn:**

- The four rules for noon sakinah and tanween, with Arabic examples
- Qalqalah, ghunnah, heavy letters and madd lengths
- How to practise so the rules become natural, and which mistakes actually matter

## Why does tajweed matter?

Allah tells the Prophet ﷺ in [Surah Al-Muzzammil, 73:4](https://quran.com/73/4):

> وَرَتِّلِ الْقُرْآنَ تَرْتِيلًا
>
> _Wa rattilil-Qurana tartila_
>
> "And recite the Quran with measured recitation."

The Companions described that recitation in detail. Anas ibn Malik was asked how the Prophet ﷺ recited, and he said it was drawn out. He then recited _Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim_, stretching _Bismillah_, _ar-Rahman_ and _ar-Rahim_ (Narrated by Bukhari, 5046). That stretching is what tajweed calls _madd_.

The Prophet ﷺ also said, "Beautify the Quran with your voices" (Narrated by Abu Dawud, 1468; graded sahih by al-Albani).

And if you find it hard, that effort counts. The Prophet ﷺ said the one who is skilled in the Quran is with the noble, righteous angels, and the one who falters and finds it difficult "will have two rewards" (Narrated by Muslim, 798).

## Before you begin

Tajweed sits on top of basic reading. Before the rules make sense, you should be able to:

- **Read Arabic letters and vowel marks** (fatha, kasra, damma, sukun, shaddah, tanween). If not, start with our guide on [how to read the Quran for beginners](/blog/how-to-read-quran-for-beginners).
- **Recognise a sukun and a shaddah** instantly, because most tajweed rules are triggered by them.
- **Know which recitation you are following.** Most printed mushafs worldwide follow _Hafs 'an 'Asim_. The rules below are for Hafs.

A few terms you'll see throughout:

- **Sakinah** — a letter carrying a sukun (no vowel)
- **Ghunnah** — a nasal sound from the nose, held for about two counts
- **Harakah / count** — the length of one short vowel, roughly the time it takes to fold one finger

## Step 1: Learn the makharij (where letters come from)

Every Arabic letter has an exit point, or _makhraj_. The five main areas are the empty space of the mouth and throat, the throat, the tongue, the lips and the nasal passage.

Focus first on the letters English speakers blur together:

- **ح and ه** — ح is a tight breathy sound from the middle of the throat; ه is a soft breath from the deepest part.
- **ع and ء** — ع comes from the middle of the throat with a squeeze; ء is a clean catch.
- **ق and ك** — ق is from the back of the tongue against the soft palate; ك is slightly further forward.
- **س, ص and ث** — ص is a heavy s; ث is a th with the tongue tip between the teeth.

**Common mistake:** treating ح as ه or ع as ء. This is not a subtle error. It changes the word, for example حَمْد (praise) becoming هَمْد.

## Step 2: Apply the rules of noon sakinah and tanween

Whenever you see a نْ or tanween (ـً ـٍ ـٌ), look at the next letter. It decides which of four rules applies.

### Izhar, idgham, iqlab and ikhfa

| Rule | Letters that trigger it | What you do | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Izhar** (clear) | ء ه ع ح غ خ | Pronounce the noon clearly, no ghunnah | مِنْ خَوْفٍ |
| **Idgham with ghunnah** | ي ن م و | Merge the noon into the next letter with a nasal hold | مَن يَقُولُ |
| **Idgham without ghunnah** | ل ر | Merge fully, no nasal sound | مِن رَّبِّهِمْ |
| **Iqlab** (switch) | ب | Turn the noon into a hidden meem with ghunnah | مِن بَعْدِ |
| **Ikhfa** (hide) | The remaining 15 letters | Half-hide the noon with ghunnah | مِن قَبْلِ |

Two points to remember:

1. **Idgham only happens across two words.** Inside one word, such as دُنْيَا, the noon is read clearly.
2. **Meem sakinah follows a similar pattern.** Before ب it is hidden with ghunnah (_ikhfa shafawi_), before another م it merges (_idgham shafawi_), and before every other letter it is read clearly (_izhar shafawi_).

## Step 3: Hold the ghunnah on noon and meem with shaddah

Any نّ or مّ with a shaddah gets a full ghunnah of about **two counts**. You hear it in إِنَّ and ثُمَّ.

**Common mistake:** rushing past the shaddah so the ghunnah disappears, or holding it for four or five counts like a song.

## Step 4: Bounce the qalqalah letters

Five letters echo slightly when they carry a sukun: **ق ط ب ج د**. Students remember them with the phrase قُطْبُ جَدٍّ.

- **Minor qalqalah** — the sukun is in the middle of a word, as in يَجْعَلُونَ. Give a small bounce.
- **Major qalqalah** — you stop on the letter at the end of a verse, as in الْفَلَقِ or أَحَدٌ. The bounce is stronger.

**Common mistake:** adding a full vowel, so أَحَدْ becomes "ahada". The bounce is an echo, not a new syllable.

## Step 5: Stretch the madd for the right length

Madd letters are ا, و and ي when they lengthen the vowel before them. The length depends on what follows.

- **Natural madd** (_madd tabi'i_) — nothing special follows. Stretch **2 counts**, as in قَالَ.
- **Connected madd** (_madd muttasil_) — a hamzah follows in the same word, as in جَاءَ. Stretch **4–5 counts**.
- **Separated madd** (_madd munfasil_) — the hamzah starts the next word, as in يَا أَيُّهَا. In Hafs by way of ash-Shatibiyyah, stretch **4–5 counts** (2 is also a valid transmission).
- **Necessary madd** (_madd lazim_) — a sukun or shaddah follows in the same word, as in الضَّالِّينَ. Stretch **6 counts**.
- **Madd before a stop** (_'arid lis-sukun_) — you stop on a word like الْعَالَمِينَ. You may stretch **2, 4 or 6 counts**.

Keep your counts consistent within one recitation. That consistency is what makes a recitation sound even.

## Step 6: Make heavy letters heavy

Seven letters are always heavy (_tafkhim_), filling the mouth with sound: **خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ**.

Two letters change:

- **The lam in "Allah"** is heavy after a fatha or damma (قَالَ اللَّهُ) and light after a kasra (بِسْمِ اللَّهِ).
- **The ra** is generally heavy with a fatha or damma and light with a kasra.

**Common mistake:** letting a heavy letter make the following alif sound light, or making every letter heavy because it "sounds more Arabic".

## Step 7: Stop and start in the right places

Stopping in the wrong place can change the meaning. The mushaf gives you signs above the text:

- **مـ** — you should stop here
- **لا** — do not stop here
- **ج** — stopping and continuing are both fine
- **صلى** — continuing is better
- **قلى** — stopping is better
- **∴ ∴** — stop at one of the two marks, not both

When you stop on a word, end it on a sukun. When you restart after catching your breath, go back a word or two so the meaning is not cut.

## How to practise tajweed every day

Knowing the rules is the easy part. Applying them without thinking takes repetition.

1. **Learn with a teacher.** Tajweed has always been passed on by listening and correction. Even one online class a week makes a real difference.
2. **Listen before you recite.** Choose one reciter with clear, measured recitation and follow along in the mushaf. The [Islamful Quran reader](/quran) lets you read the Arabic as you listen.
3. **Take one rule a week.** Spend a week only noticing noon sakinah, then qalqalah, then madd.
4. **Recite short surahs slowly.** Juz 'Amma is full of qalqalah and madd examples.
5. **Record yourself.** Play it back against the reciter and mark where you differ.

If you are also memorising, these habits pair well with our guide on [how to memorize the Quran](/blog/how-to-memorize-the-quran).

## Is tajweed obligatory?

Scholars separate recitation mistakes into two kinds. The Jordanian Dar al-Ifta explains that learning enough to avoid mistakes which change the meaning is an obligation, while observing the finer rules of letter qualities is recommended ([Dar al-Ifta Jordan](https://islamqa.org/shafii/darul-iftaa-jordan/226816/is-reciting-quran-with-tajweed-obligatory/)).

- **Lahn jali (clear error)** — changing a letter, a vowel, or a word. For example, reading ع as ء. Avoiding this is required, and neglecting to learn when you are able is sinful.
- **Lahn khafi (hidden error)** — a slightly short ghunnah, an uneven madd, or a qalqalah that is too weak. Correcting these is recommended and part of reciting beautifully.

So never stop reciting because your tajweed is not perfect. The double reward in Muslim 798 is promised to exactly the person who struggles.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Merging ح/ه and ع/ء.** This is lahn jali, because it changes words. Fix makharij first.
- **Adding a vowel to qalqalah.** The echo should not become an extra syllable.
- **Uneven madd lengths.** Stretching one connected madd to 4 counts and the next to 6 sounds careless.
- **Stopping mid-phrase.** Watch the لا sign, and restart a word or two back.
- **Chasing a melody before the letters.** The Prophet ﷺ encouraged a beautiful voice, but scholars warn against exaggeration that distorts the rules ([IslamQA](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/159897)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Do I need tajweed when reciting in salah?**
Yes, the same rules apply. At minimum, your Surah Al-Fatihah must be free of clear errors that change the meaning, because it is a pillar of the prayer. See [how to pray salah for beginners](/blog/how-to-pray-salah-for-beginners).

**Q: What is the best order to learn tajweed rules?**
Start with makharij, then noon and meem sakinah, then qalqalah and ghunnah, then madd, then heavy and light letters, and finally the stopping signs.

**Q: Is it better to recite slowly or quickly?**
Slow, measured recitation (_tartil_) is praised in Surah Al-Muzzammil, 73:4. Faster recitation (_hadr_) is allowed if every rule is still applied.

## Summary

- Learn the **makharij** first, since most serious mistakes come from mixing letters.
- Apply the four **noon sakinah** rules and the three **meem sakinah** rules.
- Hold **ghunnah** for two counts, bounce the **qalqalah** letters, and keep **madd** lengths consistent.
- Make the seven **heavy letters** heavy, and follow the **stopping signs**.
- Learn with a teacher, listen daily, and keep going. The struggle itself earns a double reward.

Allah knows best.
