![Green illustration with the title Does Swearing Break Wudu](/static/images/blog/does-swearing-break-wudu.webp)

Swearing does not break wudu. Wudu is broken by specific physical causes, and bad language is not one of them. That does not make swearing harmless: it is a sin of the tongue that needs repentance, and some people choose to renew their wudu afterwards as a fresh start. This guide explains the difference and what to do after a slip.

## The short answer

- **Wudu is not broken** by swearing, cursing or foul language. Your prayer with that wudu is valid.
- **The sin is real.** It is dealt with through repentance and better speech, not through repeating wudu.
- **Renewing wudu is optional.** You may refresh it, but you are not required to.

## What actually breaks wudu

Allah commands wudu when you rise to pray and mentions relieving yourself as a cause for purifying again ([Surah Al-Ma'idah, 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). The Prophet ﷺ said that the prayer of one who has broken wudu is not accepted until he performs ablution ([Sahih Muslim 225](https://sunnah.com/muslim:225)).

What "broken wudu" means is physical. When Abu Hurairah was asked what hadath is, he answered with an example: passing wind. Imam al-Bukhari placed this narration in a chapter titled for those who hold that wudu is repeated only for what exits the front or back passage ([Sahih al-Bukhari 176](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:176)). Darul Ifta Birmingham, a Hanafi institution, likewise states that what exits the front and back passages nullifies wudu ([Passing Wind](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/passing-wind/)).

Words are not on that list. For the full set of nullifiers and common doubts, see [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu).

## Why swearing is still serious

The Prophet ﷺ said the believer does not attack people's honour, does not curse, and is not obscene or foul ([Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1977](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1977), graded hasan). He also said that whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day should speak good or stay silent ([Sahih al-Bukhari 6018](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6018)).

So the ruling is twofold. Your purity is intact, but your tongue has done something that needs to be put right. For a fuller look at the ruling on bad language, read [is swearing haram](/blog/is-swearing-haram).

## Swearing an oath by something other than Allah

"Swearing" can also mean taking an oath. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever swears by al-Lat and al-'Uzza should say la ilaha illallah ([Sahih al-Bukhari 4860](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4860)). The remedy he gave for that serious slip was a declaration of tawhid. The narration does not instruct the person to repeat their wudu.

## What to do after you swear

1. **Stop and seek forgiveness.** Say astaghfirullah and mean it.
2. **If it was an oath by other than Allah,** say la ilaha illallah, as in the hadith above.
3. **Apologise** if your words hurt or insulted someone.
4. **Pray as normal.** Your wudu still stands unless something physical broke it.
5. **Renew wudu if you want a reset.** Ibn Kathir's commentary on 5:6, shown on [Quran.com](https://quran.com/5/6), notes that wudu before prayer is an obligation when you are impure and a recommendation when you are already pure. Refreshing it is a good act, not a requirement.

## Worked example

You make wudu for Asr, then get cut off in traffic and swear on the way to the mosque. Nothing has left your body and you have not slept. Your wudu is still valid, so you can pray Asr with it. Before you pray, say astaghfirullah. If you would like to wash again to settle your heart, you may.
