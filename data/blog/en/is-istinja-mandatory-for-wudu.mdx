![Dark green title card reading Is Istinja Mandatory for Wudu?](/static/images/blog/is-istinja-mandatory-for-wudu.webp)

Not as part of wudu. Istinja, cleaning yourself after using the toilet, is a separate act and not one of the steps of wudu. You only need it when something impure has come out. But if you have used the toilet, that impurity must be dealt with before you pray, so in practice you clean first and then make wudu.

How strong the requirement is depends on the case and on your school. Below is what the checked sources say, including where they use different labels.

## Quick answer

| Situation                                        | Istinja needed?                     |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| You urinated or passed stool                     | Yes, clean before prayer            |
| You are renewing wudu with nothing impure on you | No                                  |
| You only passed wind                             | Nothing visible to clean; see below |
| Arousal fluid (madhi) came out                   | Yes, wash then make wudu            |

## Why istinja is not a step of wudu

The verse on wudu commands washing the face, washing the hands to the elbows, wiping the head and washing the feet ([Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:6](https://quran.com/5/6)). Cleaning the private parts is not in that list. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta gives the same picture: its step-by-step guide to wudu does not include istinja, although it does say that preparing for prayer includes making sure you are clean from physical impurities ([How to perform wudu](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/249/how-to-perform-wudhu-ablution)).

So the two jobs are different. Istinja removes impurity from the body. Wudu is the washing required for prayer. For the full steps of the second, see [how to perform wudu](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu).

## Is istinja obligatory or sunnah?

This is where the labels differ, and both matter.

- **Dar al-Ifta (Egypt).** Asked whether istinja is a condition, it says istinja is obligatory for removing impurity ([fatwa 7853](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7853/is-washing-the-private-part-after-urinating-istinjaa-a-condition-and-is-it-d)).
- **Darul Ifta Birmingham (Hanafi).** It grades the act by how far the impurity has spread. Istinja is sunnah when the impurity has not spread past the exit, wajib when it has spread to the size of a dirham, and fard when it has spread further ([The Sunnah method of Istinjaah](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/the-sunnah-method-of-istinjaah/)). Another of its answers calls it an emphasised sunnah and says water becomes wajib if impurity spreads beyond a dirham ([fatwa 04898](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/is-it-permissible-to-do-istinjah-with-baby-wipes/)).

Do not read "sunnah" here as "you can skip it". The Prophet ﷺ warned about carelessness with urine. Passing two graves, he said one of the two men being punished never saved himself from being soiled with his urine ([Sahih al-Bukhari 218](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:218)).

The Hanafi service also says it is compulsory to make sure the drops of urine have stopped, by whatever method suits your habit ([same fatwa](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/the-sunnah-method-of-istinjaah/)).

This guide could not find an approved source setting out the Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali grading of istinja, so it does not state them. If you follow one of those schools, ask a scholar of that school.

## What the hadith show

The reports mention both water and stones:

- **Water.** Anas bin Malik carried a tumbler of water for the Prophet ﷺ when he went to answer the call of nature ([Sahih al-Bukhari 152](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:152)). The people of Quba, who cleansed themselves with water, are linked to the verse "In it are men who love to be purified" ([Sunan Abi Dawud 44](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:44), graded sahih by al-Albani; [Surah At-Tawbah 9:108](https://quran.com/9/108)).
- **Stones.** He said three stones are enough ([Sunan Abi Dawud 40](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:40), graded hasan by al-Albani). Salman reported that the Prophet ﷺ forbade cleaning with the right hand, with fewer than three pebbles, or with dung or bone ([Sahih Muslim 262a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:262a)).

## What can you clean with?

Dar al-Ifta says toilet paper, a stone or water all work, and that doing paper first and then water is preferable. Either alone is permissible if it removes the impurity. Soap is not a condition ([fatwa 7853](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7853/is-washing-the-private-part-after-urinating-istinjaa-a-condition-and-is-it-d)).

Darul Ifta Birmingham is more specific. After urinating, tissue or baby wipes are enough if the impurity has not spread beyond a dirham. If stool has spread beyond that size, water is required ([fatwa 04898](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/is-it-permissible-to-do-istinjah-with-baby-wipes/)).

## When you do not need it

If you are simply renewing your wudu, or it broke through something that left nothing on your body, there is nothing to clean.

Passing wind breaks wudu if you are certain it happened ([Passing Wind](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/passing-wind/)). But one Darul Ifta Birmingham answer says that in Hanafi fiqh, istinja is only required for what exits and is visible ([fatwa 09389](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/the-effect-of-discharge-bleeding-and-passing-wind-during-ghusl/)). Applying that to wind is our reading: nothing visible came out, so go straight to wudu.

## Does the order matter?

Clean first, then make wudu. That is the order in the hadith on madhi, "Perform ablution after washing your organ" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 269](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:269)). It is also what Darul Ifta Birmingham tells a woman to do after madhy ([fatwa 02816](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/when-is-a-woman-obliged-to-perform-istinja-if-she-feels-madhy/)).

Doing it the other way round raises a question about touching the private parts. Darul Ifta Birmingham says that doing so does not break wudu in the Hanafi school, but that in the Shafi'i school it does, with or without desire ([Validity of wudhu when touching one's private parts](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/validity-of-wudhu-when-touching-ones-private-parts/)). By our reading, a Shafi'i who touches the private parts while washing after wudu would need to repeat it. More in [does touching private parts break wudu?](/blog/does-touching-private-parts-break-wudu).

## Worked examples

**You used the toilet, wiped with tissue, and nothing spread.** The Dar al-Ifta and Darul Ifta Birmingham answers above both accept this. Then make wudu.

**You made wudu, then remembered you did not clean yourself after urinating.** Clean yourself now. A Hanafi does not need to repeat wudu because of the touching. None of the checked sources rule directly on the case where wudu came first, so ask a scholar of your school if you are unsure.

**You find wetness after salah.** It may be water left over from istinja. Darul Ifta Birmingham says that does not break wudu, and that doubts are not acted on without certainty ([fatwa 07475](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/finding-wetness-on-front-passage-after-salah/)).
