Maybe you are putting on hijab for the first time, or you have worn it for years and it still slips by lunchtime. Either way, it helps to separate two questions: what Islam actually asks of you, and how to make the scarf stay put.

> **Quick answer:** To wear hijab, cover your hair, ears and neck with an opaque scarf and let it fall over your chest, then wear loose clothing that does not reveal your shape. The basis is Surah An-Nur 24:31, which tells believing women "to draw their head coverings over their chests." The style, fabric and fastening are up to you.

**What you'll learn:**

- What hijab must cover, and where scholars differ
- How to wear three simple everyday styles
- The common mistakes that make a hijab fall short of the conditions

## What is hijab, and is it obligatory?

In everyday use, "hijab" means the head covering. In the Quran and in Islamic law it is broader: it describes modest dress and conduct as a whole. The scarf is one part of that.

Covering is **obligatory (fard)** for a Muslim woman once she reaches puberty. This is the position of all four Sunni schools. The main evidence is two verses.

Allah (SWT) says in Surah An-Nur, 24:31:

> وَلْيَضْرِبْنَ بِخُمُرِهِنَّ عَلَىٰ جُيُوبِهِنَّ
>
> _Wal-yadribna bi-khumurihinna 'ala juyubihinn_
>
> "And let them draw their head coverings over their chests."

A _khimar_ was the head cloth women already wore. The verse did not introduce it; it told women to bring it down over the neckline. Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) described the early Muslim women's response: when this verse was revealed, they tore their wraps and covered themselves with them (Narrated by Bukhari, 4758).

In Surah Al-Ahzab, 33:59, Allah tells the Prophet ﷺ to instruct the believing women:

> يُدْنِينَ عَلَيْهِنَّ مِن جَلَابِيبِهِنَّ
>
> _Yudnina 'alayhinna min jalabibihinn_
>
> "To draw their outer garments close around them."

You can read both verses in full with tafsir on [Quran.com, 24:31](https://quran.com/24/31) and [Quran.com, 33:59](https://quran.com/33/59), or in our [Quran reader](/quran).

## Before you begin: the conditions of hijab

Scholars drew a set of conditions from the Quran and Sunnah. The scarf is only valid hijab if the whole outfit meets them. [IslamQA's summary of the requirements](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/6991) lists them in detail. In short, your clothing should be:

- **Covering** — hair, ears, neck, chest, arms and legs are covered
- **Opaque** — no see-through fabric
- **Loose** — not tight enough to show the shape of the body
- **Not an attraction in itself** — no dress designed to draw eyes
- **Not resembling men's clothing** — the Prophet ﷺ cursed women who imitate men and men who imitate women (Narrated by Bukhari, 5885)
- **Unperfumed** when going out among non-mahram men

The Prophet ﷺ warned about "women who are clothed yet naked" (Narrated by Muslim, 2128). That describes clothing that technically covers but is so thin or tight that it defeats the purpose. It is the main thing to keep in mind when you shop.

### Do you need to cover your face?

This is the one real difference of opinion. The table sums up the classical positions.

| Position                                      | Face and hands                                                                  | Held by                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Face and hands may be uncovered               | Not required to cover; covering is praiseworthy                                  | Hanafi and Maliki schools; many Shafi'i scholars                     |
| Face should be covered in front of non-mahram | Required, especially where there is fear of temptation                          | Relied-upon Hanbali position; many later Shafi'i scholars            |
| Covering always recommended                   | Not strictly obligatory, but encouraged in times of fitnah                       | Later Hanafi scholars                                                 |

Those who do not require the face veil often cite the report that the Prophet ﷺ told Asma bint Abi Bakr that once a girl reaches puberty, nothing should be seen of her except her face and hands (Narrated by Abu Dawud, 4104). Be aware that Abu Dawud himself noted a break in its chain; al-Albani graded it sound due to supporting routes, while others consider it weak.

If you want to understand the case for the niqab, [IslamQA's answer on face covering](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/21134) explains it. Follow the position of the scholars you trust.

## How to wear hijab: step by step

These steps work for the most common style, a rectangular or large square scarf. Get the base right and every other style becomes easier.

### Step 1: Start with an underscarf

Tie your hair back low, then pull on an underscarf cap (sometimes called a bonnet or ninja cap). It covers stray hairs, stops slippery fabric from sliding, and gives pins something to grip.

**Common mistake:** a high bun under a thin scarf. It pulls the scarf backwards and exposes the front of the hairline within an hour.

### Step 2: Drape the scarf

Place the scarf over your head with the front edge sitting just past your hairline. Let one side hang longer than the other, roughly two-thirds and one-third.

### Step 3: Secure under the chin

Bring both sides together under your chin and fasten with a safety pin or a magnet. Check in the mirror that no hair shows at the forehead or temples.

### Step 4: Wrap the long side

Take the longer side across your throat, over the top of your head, and pin it behind the opposite ear. This double layer is what covers the neck properly.

### Step 5: Bring it down over your chest

Let the remaining fabric fall loosely over your chest and shoulders. This is the part the verse names directly, so do not tuck it into your collar or throw it back over your shoulder.

### Step 6: Final check

Turn side to side in the mirror. Hair, ears, neck and chest should all be covered, and you should not be able to see through the fabric in good light.

## Three easy everyday styles

Once the base is in place, the style is personal. Here are three that meet the conditions and suit different days.

| Style                   | Best for                  | How it works                                                                      | Watch out for                                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Classic wrap (rectangle) | Work, university, outings | The six steps above                                                               | Pinning too tightly at the neck                |
| Square scarf folded     | Beginners, quick mornings | Fold into a triangle, pin under the chin, cross the ends behind and bring forward | Ends that are too short to cover the chest     |
| Slip-on (al-amira)      | School, sport, travel     | A two-piece tube cap and pull-on scarf, no pins                                   | Stretchy fabric that clings to the neck        |
| Khimar                  | Prayer, fuller coverage   | A single cape-like piece that falls to the waist or below                         | Very little, apart from warmth in summer       |

For sport, a breathable slip-on style is usually safest, because pins can be a hazard and wraps come loose when you move.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Showing the neck.** Styles that leave the throat or chest bare, such as a turban with a low neckline, do not meet the wording of 24:31. Add a high-neck layer underneath.
- **Sheer fabric.** Chiffon is popular, but a single layer is often see-through. Wear it over an opaque underscarf.
- **Tight clothes with a scarf.** A hijab paired with skinny jeans and a fitted top covers the head but misses the condition of loose clothing.
- **Hijab only outside.** The obligation applies in front of non-mahram men, which includes cousins and in-laws visiting your home.
- **Heavy perfume.** Fragrance that others can notice in public is one of the listed conditions, even when the scarf itself is perfect.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q: Do you need to wear hijab when you pray alone?**
Yes. Covering the hair is a condition of a valid prayer for a woman who has reached puberty, even when nobody is watching. The Prophet ﷺ said Allah does not accept the prayer of a woman who has reached puberty without a head covering (Narrated by Abu Dawud, 641). See our guide on [how to pray salah for beginners](/blog/how-to-pray-salah-for-beginners).

**Q: Do you take hijab off to make wudu?**
The majority of scholars say you must wipe over at least part of your hair, so you would loosen the front of the scarf. Some Hanbali scholars allowed wiping over a wrapped head covering, especially in cold weather. Our [wudu guide](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu) walks through the full steps.

**Q: Can you wear makeup with hijab?**
Light, everyday makeup is discussed differently from adornment meant to attract attention. Read [is makeup haram](/blog/is-makeup-haram) for the evidence on both sides.

## Summary

Wearing hijab comes down to a few clear rules and a lot of personal choice:

- Cover your hair, ears, neck and chest with an opaque scarf, as Surah An-Nur 24:31 instructs
- Wear loose, non-transparent clothing for the rest of your body
- Scholars differ on covering the face; follow the view of scholars you trust
- Use an underscarf and a double wrap at the neck so the hijab stays in place
- Pick whichever style suits your day, as long as it meets the conditions

If it feels difficult at first, start with the simplest style and build from there. Consistency matters more than perfection, and every day you wear it for Allah is counted. For duas to recite when you leave the house, browse our [dua collection](/dua).
