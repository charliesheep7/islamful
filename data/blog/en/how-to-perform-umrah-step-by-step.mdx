Umrah is the "lesser pilgrimage": a visit to the Sacred Mosque in Makkah that, unlike Hajj, can be made in any month. It has four rites, done in a fixed order: **ihram, tawaf, sa'i, and shaving or trimming the hair.** Most pilgrims complete them in a few hours once they reach the mosque.

Allah commands in the Quran: **"Complete the pilgrimage and minor pilgrimage for Allah"** ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:196](https://quran.com/2/196)). The Prophet ﷺ said that Umrah **"is an expiation for the sins committed (between it and the previous one)"** ([Sahih al-Bukhari 1773](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1773)).

This guide gives you the rites in order, a short checklist, and the mistakes that most often trouble first-time pilgrims.

## Umrah at a Glance

| Step | Rite           | What you do                                                                    |
| ---- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1    | Ihram          | Make the intention before crossing the miqat and begin the talbiyah            |
| 2    | Tawaf          | Seven circuits around the Kaaba, starting at the Black Stone, then two rak'ahs |
| 3    | Sa'i           | Seven walks between Safa and Marwa, starting at Safa and finishing at Marwa    |
| 4    | Halq or taqsir | Men shave or shorten the hair; women trim a small amount                       |

Once the fourth step is done, the restrictions of ihram are lifted and your Umrah is complete.

## Before You Travel: A Practical Checklist

- **Check the current entry and permit rules.** Saudi visa, booking and Umrah permit requirements change. Confirm them with the official Saudi authorities or your licensed travel provider shortly before you book, not from an old blog post or a friend's experience.
- **Learn your miqat.** The Prophet ﷺ fixed boundary points (mawaqit) for pilgrims arriving from different directions. Find out which one your route passes. If you fly, airlines usually announce when the plane is approaching it, so be ready in ihram clothing beforehand.
- **Prepare your ihram.** Men need two plain unstitched sheets: one wrapped around the waist and one over the upper body. Women wear their normal modest clothing; there is no special colour or garment.
- **Groom before ihram.** Trim nails and unwanted hair and take a full bath before entering ihram. If you need a refresher, see [how to perform ghusl](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl). Any scent goes on the body before ihram, never on the ihram cloth.
- **Pack for the rites.** Comfortable sandals, a small bag for shoes inside the mosque, a refillable water bottle, any medicines you need, and unscented toiletries for the days you remain in ihram.
- **Set up your prayer tools.** Save your [prayer times](/prayer-times) for Makkah and keep the [Qibla finder](/qibla-finder) handy for travel days and your hotel room.

## Step 1: Enter Ihram at the Miqat

Ihram is the sacred state you enter with your intention. Clothing alone does not make it; the intention does.

1. **Put on your ihram clothing** before you reach the miqat.
2. **Pray two rak'ahs** if it is not a disliked time for prayer. Many scholars recommend this but it is not a condition of ihram.
3. **Make the intention for Umrah** in your heart. Many pilgrims also say: لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ عُمْرَةً (_Labbayka Allahumma 'Umrah_), "Here I am, O Allah, for Umrah."
4. **Begin the talbiyah** and repeat it often on the way to Makkah:

لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ

_Labbayka Allahumma labbayk, labbayka la sharika laka labbayk, inna al-hamda wan-ni'mata laka wal-mulk, la sharika lak._

"Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am, You have no partner, here I am. All praise, all blessings and all sovereignty belong to You. You have no partner."

This is the talbiyah of the Prophet ﷺ as narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar ([Sahih al-Bukhari 1549](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1549)). Men say it aloud; women say it so that only those near them hear.

**While in ihram, avoid:** cutting hair or nails, using perfume or scented products, hunting, marital relations, and proposing or contracting marriage. Men additionally do not wear stitched, fitted clothing or cover their heads. Women do not wear a face veil (niqab) or gloves, but may lower a cloth over the face in front of unrelated men.

## Step 2: Perform Tawaf Around the Kaaba

When you reach Masjid al-Haram, enter with your right foot and the dua for entering the mosque. Stop the talbiyah when you begin tawaf. You need to be in a state of wudu for tawaf.

1. **Start at the Black Stone corner.** Face the Black Stone and, if you can reach it without pushing anyone, kiss or touch it. Otherwise raise your right hand toward it and say _"Allahu Akbar."_ When Umar kissed it, he said: **"I know that you are a stone and can neither benefit anyone nor harm anyone. Had I not seen Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) kissing you I would not have kissed you"** ([Sahih al-Bukhari 1597](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1597)).
2. **Walk seven circuits** counter-clockwise, keeping the Kaaba on your left. Each return to the Black Stone line completes one circuit.
3. **Men walk briskly (ramal) in the first three circuits** and at a normal pace in the remaining four. Abdullah ibn Umar reported that the Prophet ﷺ did ramal in the first three rounds and walked in the remaining four, in Hajj and Umrah ([Sahih al-Bukhari 1604](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1604)). Men also uncover the right shoulder during this tawaf (idtiba'). Women walk normally throughout.
4. **Make dua in your own words.** There is no required dua for each circuit. Between the Yemeni corner and the Black Stone, many recite: _Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanah wa fil-akhirati hasanah wa qina 'adhab an-nar._ If you need ideas, browse our [dua collection](/dua).
5. **Pray two rak'ahs after tawaf**, behind Maqam Ibrahim if there is room, otherwise anywhere in the mosque. Then drink Zamzam water.

**Losing count is common in the crowd.** If you are unsure whether you have done, say, four or five circuits, count the lower number and continue from there.

## Step 3: Perform Sa'i Between Safa and Marwa

Sa'i follows tawaf. You walk between the two hills that Allah calls His symbols: **"Indeed, ˹the hills of˺ Ṣafa and Marwah are among the symbols of Allah. So whoever makes the major or minor pilgrimage to the ˹Sacred˺ House, let them walk between ˹the two hills˺"** ([Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:158](https://quran.com/2/158)).

1. **Begin at Safa.** Face the direction of the Kaaba, raise your hands, praise Allah and make dua.
2. **Walk to Marwa.** That is one lap. Walking back to Safa is the second.
3. **Continue until the seventh lap ends at Marwa.** If your count finishes at Safa, you have miscounted.
4. **Men jog lightly between the green lights** marked along the route. Women walk normally.
5. **Make dua at both hills** and along the way. As with tawaf, there is no fixed script.

Wudu is recommended for sa'i but most scholars do not make it a condition. Upper floors and wheelchair routes are available if the ground level is crowded or walking is difficult.

## Step 4: Shave or Trim the Hair

This final step releases you from ihram.

- **Men:** shaving the whole head (halq) is better, and trimming the hair evenly (taqsir) is also valid. The Prophet ﷺ prayed for mercy on those who shaved their heads, repeating it when asked about those who shortened their hair, and included them only at the third request ([Sahih al-Bukhari 1727](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1727)).
- **Women:** gather the hair and cut about a fingertip's length from the ends. Ibn Abbas narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said: **"Shaving is not a duty laid on women; only clipping the hair is incumbent on them"** ([Sunan Abi Dawud 1984](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:1984)).

After this, everything forbidden by ihram becomes permitted again. Your Umrah is complete.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Crossing the miqat without ihram.** If you do, scholars generally say you should go back to the miqat to enter ihram; if you do not, a compensation is due. Get ready before your flight or road trip reaches it.
- **Doing sa'i before tawaf.** The order is fixed: tawaf first, then sa'i.
- **Shaving or trimming too early.** Do not cut the hair until sa'i is finished.
- **Women shaving the head.** Women only trim a small amount.
- **Trimming only a few strands.** Men who choose taqsir should shorten the hair across the head, not just one spot.
- **Pushing to reach the Black Stone.** Touching it is sunnah; harming others is not permitted. Pointing toward it is enough.
- **Fixating on printed duas.** Sincere supplication in a language you understand is better than reading words you do not follow.

## Where Scholars Differ

- **Is Umrah obligatory?** Scholars hold two main positions: that it is obligatory once in a lifetime for those able, or that it is a strongly recommended sunnah. Both agree it is highly rewarded.
- **Women travelling for Umrah.** Classical scholars differ on whether a woman may travel for Umrah without a mahram. Current Saudi entry rules are a separate, changing question. Check both with a scholar you trust and with official sources before you book.
- **Small details.** Schools differ on details such as the minimum amount of hair to cut and what compensation is due for specific violations. If something goes wrong during your Umrah, ask a qualified scholar before trying to fix it yourself.

## After Umrah

You may perform tawaf as often as you like during your stay; voluntary tawaf has no sa'i or hair-cutting. Keep up your five daily prayers in the Haram, and if you plan the larger pilgrimage later, read our [step-by-step Hajj guide](/blog/how-to-perform-hajj-step-by-step).
