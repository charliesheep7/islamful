Yes. You can do ruqyah without wudu. Ruqyah means reciting Quran and making dua over yourself or someone who is unwell, and the fatwas checked here allow reciting Quran from memory without wudu. Two limits apply: touching a printed mushaf, and being in a state of major ritual impurity (janabah).

## Quick answer

| Situation                                                      | Ruqyah without wudu?                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Reciting Al-Fatiha, Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas from memory | Yes                                           |
| Making dua for healing                                         | Yes                                           |
| Blowing over yourself or a sick person after reciting          | Yes                                           |
| Reading from a copy of the Quran without touching it           | Yes, according to Dar al-Ifta                 |
| Holding and reading from a printed mushaf                      | Make wudu first, according to the same fatwas |
| In janabah                                                     | Do not recite Quran; dua is still open        |

## What ruqyah looks like in the Sunnah

The clearest model is the Prophet's ﷺ own practice. Aisha reported that when anyone in his household fell ill, he blew over them while reciting the Mu'awwidhat, and that during his final illness she blew over him and wiped him with his own hand ([Sahih Muslim 2192](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2192)). Every night when he went to bed, he cupped his hands, blew into them after reciting Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, and wiped over as much of his body as he could, starting with his head and face. He did this three times ([Sahih al-Bukhari 5017](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5017)).

A group of companions on a journey also recited Al-Fatiha over a tribal chief who had been stung, and he got up as if released from a chain. The Prophet ﷺ approved, asking how they knew Al-Fatiha could be used as ruqyah ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2276](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2276)). The Quran itself is described as "a healing and mercy for the believers" ([Surah Al-Isra 17:82](https://quran.com/17/82)).

None of these three narrations mentions wudu as part of the ruqyah, either to require it or to rule it out.

## Why wudu is not required

Ruqyah is made of Quran recitation and dua, so it follows the ruling on reciting Quran. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta says there is no objection to reciting the Quran without ablution, or reading from a copy of it, provided one does not touch it. It adds that the Prophet ﷺ recited the Quran in any state except major ritual impurity ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 7835](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7835/is-it-permissible-to-recite-the-quran-without-ablution); the same answer appears in [fatwa 1378](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/1378/reciting-the-quran-without-ablution)).

Darul Ifta Birmingham, a Hanafi fatwa service, answers the exact ruqyah case of the four Quls and Ayat al-Kursi: reciting without touching the Quran is permitted in a state of minor ritual impurity and not permitted in major ritual impurity ([Darul Ifta Birmingham, fatwa 02148](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/?p=9030)).

In practice, if your wudu has broken through using the toilet, sleep or passing wind, you can still recite ruqyah over yourself or your child from memory. Our guide to [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu) explains the minor-impurity cases.

## Where views differ: touching the mushaf

Dar al-Ifta grounds the touching rule in the verse "None touch it except the purified" (Quran 56:79). That verse is read in more than one way. The Clear Quran translation on Quran.com renders it as "touched by none except the purified ˹angels˺" ([Surah Al-Waqi'ah 56:79](https://quran.com/56/79)), which takes it as a description of the record kept with Allah rather than a rule for people.

Dar al-Ifta's own wording also varies. A third fatwa says that whoever wants to read from the Quran must be purified from both minor and major impurity, citing a narration from Ali, and then says there is nothing wrong with reading from memory without touching it ([Dar al-Ifta fatwa 7829](https://dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/7829/is-it-permissible-to-read-the-quran-without-making-wudu-ablution)). All three Dar al-Ifta answers agree on the point that matters most for ruqyah: recitation from memory needs no wudu.

If you want to read longer passages from a printed mushaf, make wudu before picking it up. Reading on a screen is a separate question, covered in [reading Quran on a phone without wudu](/blog/can-you-read-quran-on-phone-without-wudu).

## Ruqyah in janabah

Both fatwa services exclude major ritual impurity. If you are junub, take a ghusl before reciting Quran as ruqyah. Until then, you can still ask Allah for healing in your own words. See [dhikr and dua without wudu](/blog/dhikr-and-dua-without-wudu) for what remains open in janabah and during menstruation.

## A simple routine

1. Make wudu if it is easy. The sources above do not make it a condition, but nothing stops you from doing it.
2. Recite Al-Fatiha, then Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas.
3. Blow lightly into your cupped hands and wipe over your head, face and body, or blow over the person who is ill, as in Bukhari 5017 and Muslim 2192.
4. Repeat it three times, following the Prophet's ﷺ bedtime practice.
5. Ask Allah for healing in your own words.
