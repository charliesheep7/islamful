Yes, you can read the Quran on your phone without wudu. Reciting the Quran without wudu is allowed in the first place, and touching a phone screen that shows Quran text is not treated like touching a printed mushaf. The one thing a phone does not change is janabah: if you are in that state, make ghusl before you recite.

This guide is about the phone itself: scrolling, tapping, holding it in your hand, and reading in spare moments during the day. For the wider rules on reciting from memory and handling a printed copy, see [Can you read Quran without wudu?](/blog/can-you-read-quran-without-wudu).

## Why reciting without wudu is allowed

The Prophet ﷺ recited the Quran before making wudu. Ibn Abbas stayed overnight in the house of his aunt Maimuna and saw the Prophet wake in the middle of the night, rub the sleep from his face, recite the last ten verses of Surah Al Imran, and only then get up to make wudu and pray ([Sahih al-Bukhari 183](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:183)). Imam al-Bukhari placed this report under a chapter on reciting the Quran after hadath.

Ali ibn Abi Talib also reported that the Prophet ﷺ would recite the Quran in all conditions, as long as he was not junub ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 146](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:146)). And A'ishah said that he used to remember Allah at all times ([Sahih Muslim 373](https://sunnah.com/muslim:373)).

So the act of reading or reciting is not the issue. The question people actually have is about touching.

## Touching a screen is not touching a mushaf

After reporting Ali's hadith, at-Tirmidhi records the position of many scholars among the Companions and those after them: a person may recite the Quran without wudu, but should not read from the mushaf except while pure. He names Sufyan ath-Thawri, ash-Shafi'i, Ahmad and Ishaq as holding this view ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 146](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:146)).

A phone is not a mushaf. Darul Ifta Birmingham was asked directly whether touching the screen while reading the Quran on a mobile phone needs wudu. Its answer explains that the text on a screen is made of tiny lit pixels, never solid like ink on paper, and that the command for wudu applies to the permanent written words of the Quran in a mushaf or on a page. It adds that a glass layer sits above the display, so you never touch the text directly. Its conclusion: touching the screen while the Quran is displayed does not require wudu ([Darul Ifta Birmingham, fatwa 05038](https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/will-it-be-permissible-to-touch-the-mobile-phone-screen-when-reading-the-quran-without-wudhu/)).

The same fatwa ends with advice worth keeping: a believer should ideally always be in a state of wudu, even when it is not required.

### What about Surah Al-Waqi'ah 56:79?

This verse is often mentioned in this topic. It describes the Quran as being in a well-preserved Record, "touched by none except the purified" ([Surah Al-Waqi'ah 56:77-80](https://quran.com/56/77-80)). The Clear Quran translation on Quran.com reads "the purified" as the angels ([56:79](https://quran.com/56/79)). Because of that interpretive question, this guide relies on the jurists' recorded position about the mushaf rather than drawing a phone ruling from the verse alone.

## When the phone does not change the ruling

Janabah is a different state from simply not having wudu. The hadith of Ali makes the exception explicit: the Prophet ﷺ recited in all conditions _except_ when junub ([Jami at-Tirmidhi 146](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:146)). Reading from a screen instead of a book does not remove that exception. Make ghusl first; our [step-by-step ghusl guide](/blog/how-to-perform-ghusl) walks through it.

Menstruation is a separate question on which scholars differ. The [main guide on reading Quran without wudu](/blog/can-you-read-quran-without-wudu) covers it.

## Practical situations

- **Scrolling or tapping a Quran app:** allowed without wudu, according to the fatwa above.
- **Reading on the bus, at work or in bed:** allowed without wudu. This is one of the easiest ways to keep a daily portion when wudu is not convenient.
- **Listening to recitation on your phone:** no wudu needed, because listening involves no touching at all.
- **After janabah:** make ghusl before reciting, whether from a phone or a mushaf.
- **Before salah:** wudu is required for the prayer itself, not because of your phone. Use our [wudu guide](/blog/how-to-perform-wudu) if you want a refresher.

## A simple daily habit

1. Keep the [Islamful Quran reader](/quran) open on your phone for short readings during the day.
2. When you renew wudu for the next prayer, read a little more while you are in that state. Check the next prayer time with our [prayer times tool](/prayer-times).
3. If you are unsure whether something has broken your wudu, our guide to [what breaks wudu](/blog/what-breaks-wudu) lists the main cases.

If your local scholar or madhab teaches a stricter position, follow it. The point of the ruling is not to keep you away from the Quran, but to make it easy to stay close to it.

Allah knows best.
